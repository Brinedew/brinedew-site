James Phipps, the son of a poor, landless gardener, was only eight years old when his father signed him up for a risky experimental procedure to be performed by his employer, the British physician Edward Jenner. It is unclear whether his father fully understood what he was consenting to, or whether he had simply been persuaded by a payment.

What Jenner did next changed medicine forever. He inoculated the boy with material taken from a cowpox lesion, then later exposed him to smallpox. The boy did not fall ill, and the idea of vaccination was born.
