## Judicial opinion: `Non-technical language in longevity` (List → FL)

**Ruling: remand.** The page's core idea is right, and other pages already rely on it. That is exactly why it can't be promoted yet. The court rubric (`Wiki Assessment.md`, Nontechnical-language tripwire) tells every Court to judge other pages against this page's replacement terms, naming `mortality creep` and `frailty` explicitly. Two of its seven vocabulary rows are wrong, and one of them contradicts the wiki's own Glossary and a neighbor page it links to. Making it a Featured List would make those errors the standard for the whole wiki.

### What I checked myself (neither counsel could)

1. **The "Frailty" row contradicts the wiki.** `Glossary.md` defines **Frailty** as "a fixed multiplier of one person's mortality rate relative to the average person's". That is the demographers' meaning (Vaupel). `Mortality rate models.md` uses the same meaning in its gamma-Gompertz section and its Figure 6 slider ("Frailty variance γ"). The candidate's Row 1 defines frailty as "progressive physiological and anatomical impairment" at the organismal level. A page that exists to stop cross-level word confusion swaps "aging" for a word that already means something different two clicks away. Alex was right to flag frailty as overloaded. The actual defect is worse than his brief shows: it is an internal contradiction in the wiki, not just a clash with outside fields.
2. **"Mortality creep" appears nowhere in the wiki except this page and the rubric tripwire that copies it.** `Mortality rate models` (B), which Row 2 links to, uses **Gompertz slope** *b* and **doubling time** (ln 2/*b*). The Advocate says the term deliberately avoids the word "senescence". That reason isn't enough to invent a term with no standing in the literature when the linked canonical page already uses "Gompertz slope" / "age-specific rise in mortality rate". Alex says the phrase means the opposite in actuarial finance. I did not verify that, and I don't need it to rule.
3. **The FL standard in the rubric is concrete, and the page fails it as written.** It requires "explicit inclusion/ranking criteria with primary citations on every entry".
   - The page states no inclusion criteria.
   - Three of the seven vocabulary rows (Frailty, Mortality creep, Negligible senescence) have no citation at all.
   - The Senescence row cites Hayflick 1961 and Coppé 2008 for p16/p21 and SA-β-gal markers. Neither paper is the source for those markers.
4. **"Permanent" vs "stable".** `cellular-senescence.md` defines the state as a "stable cell-cycle arrest". The candidate says "permanent". The canonical page's wording should win.
5. **The broken `[[rna\|RNA interference]]` link is real.** No `rna*.md` exists in `content/wiki`. The other escaped `\|` links in the tables all resolve.
6. **Rendering is clean**: no horizontal scroll at desktop or at 390 px, and no console errors. The mobile top-of-page screenshot reads well. Rendering does not block promotion.

### Weighing the briefs

**Alex is right about:**
- **The level schemes don't line up.** The page uses molecule/cell/organ/cohort in the lead, genes/cells/personal/population in the vital-signs paragraph, Levels 1–4 in the evolutionary table, and an unnumbered Organismal/Demographic/Cellular/Tissue column in the vocabulary table. The prose also says "Level 2/Level 3" before the table that defines those numbers. The real fix is not one merged hierarchy. The page should say once that there are two axes: *units of selection* (the numbered Levels 1–4) and *measurement levels* (cell → tissue → organism → cohort, where a cohort is never a unit of selection). Then the vocabulary table's Level column should map onto that statement explicitly.
- **The lead promises entries the table doesn't have.** It names "damage" and "health", but neither has a row. Either add the rows or drop them from the lead's list. "Damage" can get a row that says it splits by level: a molecular lesion at Levels 1–2 versus an active brake (see Failure 3). The Advocate's reason for leaving it out is fair, but that reason belongs in the stated inclusion criteria, not in silence.
- **Bodnar 1998 is cited for more than it shows.** It supports cell immortalization only. The organismal sign-flip clause (telomerase removing a restraint on tumors) needs its own human evidence. The Advocate's Kim et al. 1994 and Haycock et al. 2017 (*JAMA Oncology*, Mendelian randomization) are good candidates.
- **The CTVT/germline sentence conflates cells with lineages.** It should say *lineages* of dividing cells need not deteriorate, because selection and bottlenecks purge damaged cells. It should not say *dividing cells* don't deteriorate. Baez-Ortega reports a heavy mutation load in CTVT, and the sentence should not imply otherwise.
- **The organismal row (Row 3 of the evolutionary table) attaches the insect-policing perspective [^6] to "reciprocity, reputational sanctions".** Those terms either need a source or should be cut.
- **The *A. m. capensis* placement is wrong.** Clonal workers that invade other colonies are Level-3 cheaters, a "social cancer" much like CTVT is at Level 2. They belong in the cheater column, not as evidence of weak Level-4 boundaries. Unicolonial supercolonies can stay in the superorganismal row, with a proper source (Helanterä et al. 2009).
- **Allele B:** fibrosis is pro-tumor in liver and lung. Drop "fibrotic", or limit it to contexts where the stroma restrains tumors. Keep "less arrested".
- **Allele A:** the closing summary calls the allele a change in "baseline hazard level". It actually builds a step jump at age 50. Restate it plainly. Also give the regime: the lifespan gain is large only when early-life mortality is high, which both counsels' numbers agree on.
- **Housekeeping:**
  - Fix the ISBN in [^2] (978-0-7167-4525-9 for the 1995 Freeman edition).
  - Complete [^8]: 6(12):e301.
  - Expand EDAC, piRNA and the Yamanaka factors on first use.
  - Remove See-also entries already linked in the body. Add `Clonal governance` and `Mortality rate models`.
  - Cut the line about what researchers supposedly "expect" from cell aging; it argues against a strawman.

**The Advocate is right about:**
- **The page should stay a tight lookup table.** It should not grow into a 25-row gerontology dictionary or a survey of aging theories. Healthspan, clocks and inflammaging are not required for FL. The gap is the *stated criteria* plus the two terms the lead promises.
- **A compact evolutionary-levels table can stay** as the key that defines the level numbers. It is short and delegates detail to its links. Alex's "cut the whole table" overreaches. Trim it and correct it; don't remove it.
- **Narrowing negligible senescence to a flat mortality hazard is good level discipline.** Add one clause on the cohort-vs-individual frailty trap: a cohort's hazard can flatten through selective attrition while every individual's hazard keeps rising. Mortality rate models already explains this, so link to it rather than rebuilding it.
- **Keeping the tumor-suppressor framing is legitimate.** It is the wiki's lens, it is credited by name, and it is hedged with "often". Failure 3 should still acknowledge that passive wear is real in post-mitotic tissue, in one clause.
- **Senescence-marker nuance belongs on `cellular-senescence`.** Here it only needs wording that doesn't claim the markers are sufficient, plus correct sources.

### Required fixes before re-nomination
1. Replace the Row 1 entry "Frailty" with an organismal term that doesn't collide with the wiki, e.g. **physiological decline / loss of functional reserve**. Point clinical frailty to its own term so it isn't confused with demographic frailty (the Glossary's sense).
2. Replace "mortality creep" with **Gompertz slope / age-specific rise in mortality rate (doubling time)**, matching `Mortality rate models`. In the same change, update the Wiki Assessment tripwire's examples (`mortality creep`, `frailty`).
3. Add an explicit inclusion-criteria sentence. Add Damage and Health rows, or trim the lead's list to match the table.
4. State the two axes (units of selection vs measurement levels) once, early. Make the vocabulary table's Level column map onto it.
5. Put a primary citation on every vocabulary row, and correct the source pairings: senescence markers, the reputational-sanctions claim, the supercolony and *capensis* claims, the human sign flip for TERT.
6. Make the scientific corrections above: lineage vs cell, capensis placement, the Allele B fibrosis wording, the Allele A summary and regime, "stable" instead of "permanent", and a passive-wear clause in Failure 3.
7. Fix or remove the `rna` link, fix [^2] and [^8], expand acronyms, and clean up See also.

The work is bounded: about a day of edits, with no change to the structure. Once the vocabulary rows agree with the Glossary and Mortality rate models, and every entry carries a matching source, this should pass as a Featured List.
