## Evolution (Featured Article)

> **Evolution** is the change in the heritable characteristics of biological populations over successive generations. It occurs when evolutionary processes such as genetic drift and natural selection act on genetic variation, resulting in certain characteristics becoming more or less common within a population over successive generations. The process of evolution has given rise to biodiversity at every level of biological organisation.

## Immune system (Featured Article)

> The **immune system** is a network of biological systems that protects an organism from diseases. It detects and responds to a wide variety of pathogens, such as viruses, bacteria, and parasites, as well as cancer cells and foreign objects — distinguishing them from the organism's own healthy tissue. Many species have two major subsystems of the immune system: the innate immune system provides a preconfigured response to broad groups of stimuli, while the adaptive immune system provides a tailored response by learning to recognize molecules it has previously encountered.

## RNA interference (Featured Article)

> **RNA interference** (RNAi) is a biological process in which RNA molecules are involved in sequence-specific suppression of gene expression by double-stranded RNA, through translational or transcriptional repression. Historically, RNAi was known by other names, including co-suppression, post-transcriptional gene silencing (PTGS), and quelling. The detailed study of each of these seemingly different processes elucidated that the RNAi response is triggered by double-stranded RNA, and the core mechanism is conserved across eukaryotes.

## Serpin (Featured Article)

> **Serpins** (serine protease inhibitors) are a superfamily of proteins with similar structures that were first identified for their protease inhibition activity. The acronym serpin was coined because many serpins inhibit chymotrypsin-like serine proteases. These proteins are notable for their unique mechanism of action: they present a reactive center loop as a bait substrate that, once cleaved by the target protease, undergoes a dramatic conformational change to irreversibly distort and inactivate the protease.

## DNA nanotechnology (Featured Article)

> **DNA nanotechnology** involves forming artificial, designed nanostructures out of nucleic acids, such as this DNA tetrahedron. In this field, nucleic acids are used as non-biological engineering materials for nanotechnology rather than as carriers of genetic information in living cells. The well-understood base-pairing rules (A binds T, C binds G) make DNA a useful building block, and the field has expanded to incorporate other nucleic acids such as RNA and synthetic analogs.
