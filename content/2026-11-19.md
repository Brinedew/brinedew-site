Some more info for wiki: ### The Short Verdict (as of Late 2026)

Yes—both hypotheses have established names (each actually refers to a *family* of theories), **neither is definitively settled as the sole mechanism of aging**, and the field is currently in an unusual state of **partial empirical revolution combined with heavy siloed "talking past each other."**

In brief:
* **Naive Hypothesis A** (random somatic point mutations accumulating until cells lose function) has **lost significant ground** since 2021 due to human hypermutator studies.
* **Revised Hypothesis A** (unrepaired physical *DNA lesions*—not fixed point mutations—stalling transcription and corrupting chromatin/epigenetics) is **holding strong**, especially for non-dividing organs like the brain and heart.
* **Hypothesis B** (genomic instability producing hyper-competitive "selfish" cell lineages that threaten multicellular cohesion, driving both direct pathology and evolved, costly tumor-suppressive shutdowns) has **surged empirically and theoretically**—yet remains largely sidelined by mainstream translational longevity research because its implications are therapeutically uncomfortable.

### 1. Do These Hypotheses Have Names as of 2026?

One subtle reason people talk past each other starts with terminology. In your framing of Hypothesis B, you wrote *"increasing cell functionality."* Biologists avoid that phrase because it suggests a cell gets better at serving its organ. Instead, they distinguish **tissue-serving functionality** from **cell-autonomous Darwinian fitness** (proliferative vigor, stress survival, resistance to apoptosis—i.e., *cheating* or *selfish* cells). A mutation that makes a cell "better at being a unicellular replicator" degrades **multicellular cohesion**.

With that precision in place, here is how both hypotheses are named in the 2026 literature:

| **Hypothesis B** *(Genomic instability → hyper-competitive cells → cohesion loss → degenerative adaptations → mortality)* | **1. > 1. **Hyperfunction Theory** (Mikhail Blagosklonny; David Gems): Argues cells become destructively hyperfunctional, *not* because of genomic instability, but because developmental growth programs (like mTOR) fail to switch off in adulthood.
> 2. **Adaptive Oncogenesis** (James DeGregori & Andrii Rozhok): Reverses the causal arrow—arguing that *aging-induced tissue degradation comes first*, degrading the microenvironment and thereby creating selective pressure that favors oncogenic mutants.

### 2. Scoreboard as of 2026: Who Is Winning, Who Is Losing?

Between 2021 and 2026, five major empirical findings reshaped this debate. Taken together, they **demolished the naive version of Hypothesis A**, **fortified a revised version of Hypothesis A**, and **delivered major wins—plus one unexpected twist—for Hypothesis B**.

#### 1. The Human Hypermutator Bombshell (*Loss for Naive A; Win for B*)
In a landmark *Nature Genetics* study, Robinson et al. (2021) sequenced normal tissues from humans born with germline DNA polymerase proofreading defects (*POLE* and *POLD1*), followed by biallelic *MUTYH* repair defects (2022). These individuals accumulate vastly elevated somatic point-mutation burdens (often several-fold to >10× normal; similarly, *Pms2*−/− mismatch-repair-deficient mice accumulate ~100× normal mutation rates) in normal somatic cells starting in early life.
* **The shock:** Outside of severe early cancer predisposition, these humans **do not exhibit overt premature aging**.
* **The takeaway:** As Robinson et al. explicitly wrote, human tissues can tolerate ubiquitously elevated point-mutation loads without widespread organ collapse. Reviews in 2024–2026 (e.g., Chatsirisupachai & de Magalhães, *Ageing Res Rev* 2024; Trastus & d'Adda di Fagagna, *Aging* Aug 2026) cite this as the sharpest blow against classical point-mutation Hypothesis A.

#### 2. The Progeria Dissociation (*Win for Revised Lesion-Based A + DDR-Driven B*)
Flip the experiment around: humans with Cockayne Syndrome (*CSB*/*ERCC6*) or Trichothiodystrophy (*XPD*), and *Ercc1*−/Δ mice, suffer catastrophic accelerated aging across almost every organ—**yet they often have normal or near-normal point-mutation rates and do *not* necessarily have elevated cancer**.
* **Why?** Their defect isn't replication fidelity—it's **transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair (TC-NER)**. Physical, bulky DNA *lesions* stay stuck on the DNA strand, physically stalling RNA Polymerase II—disproportionately choking long genes involved in neuronal, metabolic, and immune maintenance (**GLTD**, Gyenis et al., *Nature Genetics* 2023)—while chronically firing the DNA Damage Response (DDR) to push cells into apoptosis and senescence.
* **The takeaway:** *Unrepaired DNA lesions* directly wreck cell functionality (Revised Hyp A) *and* trigger senescence/SASP (bridging into Hyp B2).

#### 3. The Mammalian Scaling Law (*The Rorschach Test Claimed by Both A and B*)
In *Nature* (2022), Cagan, Martincorena, and colleagues sequenced 208 intestinal crypts across 16 mammalian species spanning a **~39,000-fold** range in body mass and a **~30-fold** range in lifespan. Somatic substitution rates per year scaled almost perfectly as $1/\text{lifespan}$ (from **47/yr** in humans to **796/yr** in mice), so that at the end of life every mammal converged on roughly **~3,200 substitutions per crypt** (only a ~2.9-fold spread!). Astonishingly, giraffes (**99/yr**, lifespan 24 yr, ~800 kg) and naked mole-rats (**93/yr**, lifespan 25 yr, ~35 g) had virtually identical mutation rates despite a **23,000-fold mass difference**.
* **How Hyp A reads it:** Evolution tunes genome maintenance to keep cumulative sequence/epigenetic erosion below a fatal functional ceiling (~3,200 mutations). Boosted in Jan 2025 by **Koch & Ideker (*Nature Aging*)**, who showed that somatic CpG mutations actively remodel surrounding DNA methylation—meaning "epigenetic aging clocks" may be downstream scars of somatic mutations.
* **How Hyp B reads it:** Why didn't *body mass* predict mutation rates after controlling for lifespan? Because large animals solve Peto's Paradox via *extra tumor-suppressive adaptations* (e.g., extra *TP53* copies, telomerase repression, stricter tissue architectures), whereas selection lowers mutation rates across lifespan primarily to delay **oncogenic clonal sweeps** past reproductive age.

#### 4. Non-Malignant "Selfish Clones" Killing Without Cancer (*Major Win for Hyp B*)
The cleanest real-world confirmation of Hypothesis B's *cohesion-loss* mechanism is **Clonal Hematopoiesis of Indeterminate Potential (CHIP)**. Somatic mutations in genes like *TET2*, *DNMT3A*, *ASXL1*, and *JAK2* give hematopoietic stem cells a *cell-autonomous fitness advantage*, letting mutant clones expand to dominate aging blood (present in >10–24% of adults over 70).
* **Crucially:** Most carriers never get leukemia—yet large 2024–2026 cohorts (UK Biobank, ARIC, WHI, *European Heart Journal* 2025) confirm CHIP increases **all-cause and cardiovascular mortality by ~24–40%**. Mutant progeny macrophages infiltrate arterial plaques and organs in a hyper-inflammatory state, destabilizing plaques and driving heart failure.
* The same pattern appeared in chronic liver disease: positively selected clones (e.g., *FOXO1*, *CIDEB*, *GPAM* mutations) survive lipotoxicity better at the *cell* level, while impairing systemic glucose metabolism (worsening organismal insulin resistance).
* Meanwhile, a May 2026 Sanger/DKFZ preprint (Milsom, Cagan, Martincorena) showed that **stem cell dormancy**—literally throttling regenerative activity—is a primary evolved barrier restricting blood stem-cell mutagenesis with age. That aligns directly with Hypothesis B's "degenerative adaptation" prediction.

#### 5. The *NOTCH1* Esophagus Twist (*Complicating Pure Hyp B*)
Single-cell genotype-to-phenotype mapping in normal aged human esophagus (Martincorena; Philip Jones; Dan Landau 2025–2026) revealed that by age 60, **up to 80% of histologically normal esophageal epithelium is replaced by positively selected mutant clones** (*NOTCH1*, *TP53*, etc.). Yet the tissue remains structurally normal—and remarkably, *NOTCH1* mutant clones **epistatically suppress cancer** by outcompeting nascent tumor cells. Somatic selection of "hyper-competitive" clones is therefore not *automatically* fatal to organismal cohesion; sometimes evolution even co-opts clonal competition as a firewall!

### 3. The Discourse State in 2026: Debate, Silence, or Talking Past Each Other?

Honestly, it is **all three—depending on which sub-community you walk into**:

#### A. Why People Are Talking Past Each Other
1. **Conflating Four Physically Different Things Under "Genomic Instability"**
   * When molecular gerontologists (Hoeijmakers, Schumacher, Vijg) defend **Hypothesis A**, they increasingly mean **unrepaired DNA lesions** stalling transcription (GLTD) or **chromatin/epigenetic remodeling** triggered by breaks and CpG mutations.
   * When cancer geneticists and skeptics (Robinson, Chatsirisupachai, de Magalhães) attack **Hypothesis A**, they point to **fixed base substitutions** (*POLE*/*POLD1*).
   * Both sides are empirically right about their own substrate, yet they frequently argue as if they are testing the same variable.

2. **The Mitotic vs. Post-Mitotic Blind Spot**
   * **Hypothesis B** (especially strong versions like Wolf's TSTA) explains *proliferative tissues* brilliantly—blood, gut, skin, liver, lung, where stem-cell divisions fuel clonal evolution, cancer, telomere shortening, and stem-cell exhaustion.
   * Its glaring vulnerability is **post-mitotic organs**—neurons and cardiomyocytes barely divide, rarely become tumors, yet still show robust age-related decline, DNA lesion accumulation, and GLTD. Hypothesis B advocates counter that systemic inflammation from mitotic compartments (e.g., SASP, CHIP-mutant microglia/macrophages infiltrating the heart and brain) plus evolved tissue-wide constraints explain post-mitotic decline, while Hypothesis A advocates regard post-mitotic aging as decisive proof that cell-intrinsic damage directly impairs function.

#### B. Why Mainstream Geroscience Barely Debates This
In mainstream aging biology—anchored by the influential **Hallmarks of Aging** framework (López-Otín et al., 2013 & 2023)—*"Genomic Instability," "Telomere Attrition," "Cellular Senescence,"* and *"Stem Cell Exhaustion"* are placed side by side as co-equal boxes. That diplomatic consensus lets labs develop senolytics, NAD+ boosters, or Yamanaka-factor partial reprogramming without ever forcing a verdict on **causal hierarchy**.

There is also a sociological elephant in the room, explicitly pointed out by Alexander Wolf in his 2026 TSTA monograph and echoed by Ideker & Cummings in 2025:
* **If aging is reversible epigenetic drift or mTOR hyperfunction**, then cheap small-molecule drugs or periodic cellular reprogramming can reverse it.
* **If Hypothesis B (TSTA) is true**—that aging is an *evolved, optimized firewall against somatic mutations and selfish clonal sweeps*—then pharmacologically disabling aging hallmarks (reactivating telomerase, overriding senescence, boosting stem-cell proliferation) risks slamming straight into an **oncogenic wall**, leaving **cell/organ replacement** or radical genome-stability engineering as the only true escape. That conclusion is deeply unpopular in a biotech ecosystem built around longevity pills.

### 4. Where the Best Synthesis Is Converging in 2026

If you strip away disciplinary turf wars, the strongest emerging synthesis is **partitioned by both damage type and tissue architecture**:

1. **For *fixed somatic mutations* in *proliferative tissues* (blood, epithelia, liver):** **Hypothesis B is winning decisively.** Random point mutations rarely break enough housekeeping genes simultaneously to collapse an organ (*POLE*/*POLD1* proves tissues are deeply buffered against loss-of-function mutations). Instead, their lethal leverage comes from **positive selection of selfish clones**—driving cancer, non-malignant inflammatory death (CHIP), and the evolutionary necessity of **degenerative safeguards** (telomere limits, p16/p53 senescence, stem-cell dormancy).
2. **For *unrepaired physical DNA lesions* in *post-mitotic tissues* (brain, heart, skeletal muscle):** **Revised Hypothesis A remains indispensable.** Here, transcriptional stalling of long genes (GLTD), double-strand breaks, and mutation-seeded epigenetic remodeling (Koch & Ideker 2025) erode cell functionality directly—even though Hypothesis B still leaks *into* these organs systemically via infiltrating inflammatory CHIP macrophages and SASP signaling.
3. **Evolutionarily, both sit on the same Pareto frontier:** As Nelson & Masel's model showed, multicellularity creates an inescapable trade-off. If you raise intercellular competition to clear sluggish, Hypothesis-A cells, you accelerate the takeover of selfish Hypothesis-B cells; if you suppress competition and lock down division to stop Hypothesis-B cells, you inherit degenerative tissue exhaustion. Most of the 2026 debate is no longer about whether both forces exist—it is about **which side of the vise actually limits human lifespan first**, and whether medicine can pry the vise open without triggering cancer.
