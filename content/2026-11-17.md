Info to update wiki:

The short answer is **no single method in 2026 completely rendered Y2H, co-IP, and BioID/TurboID obsolete**. Each of those classics asks a fundamentally different question (pairwise binding potential vs. stable biochemical complexes vs. live-cell spatial neighborhoods), so all three—especially co-IP for orthogonal validation and TurboID for routine proximity mapping—remain widely used workhorses.

However, **2026 (building on late 2025) brought major breakthroughs that directly solve the biggest flaws of BioID/TurboID, co-IP, and Y2H**. In several specialized contexts, next-generation tools are actively starting to displace them.

---

### 1. Challenging BioID / TurboID: "Zero-Radius" & Tiny-Tag Proximity Labeling
The core weaknesses of BioID/TurboID are (1) a ~10 nm "cloud" of diffusible biotin-AMP that tags bystander proteins, (2) high background from endogenous biotin (especially in tissues like brain), and (3) a bulky ~35 kDa enzyme tag that can disrupt sensitive bait proteins. Several 2026 advances hit these exact bottlenecks:

* **FlexID ("Zero-Radius" Proximity Labeling — Ting Lab, *bioRxiv* Sept 2026 / accepted at *Nature*):** Computationally engineered from *E. coli* lipoic acid ligase (LplA) using protein language models and inverse folding models. Instead of releasing a diffusible reactive intermediate like BioID/TurboID or APEX2, FlexID transfers **non-biotin, click-compatible small-molecule probes** via a **direct-contact ("zero-radius") mechanism**. It labels in 1–5 minutes in cells (30 minutes in live mouse brain), eliminates endogenous biotin background, and achieves significantly higher spatial specificity than TurboID and APEX2.
* **FlAsH-ID (6-Amino-Acid Tag — Seath Lab, *Nature Chemical Biology*, Nov 2026):** Rather than fusing a bulky 27–35 kDa enzyme, FlAsH-ID requires only a **6-amino-acid tetracysteine tag** paired with the small-molecule dye FlAsH-EDT₂, repurposed as a green-light photocatalyst. In primary CD8⁺ T cells, tagging the transcription factor RUNX3 with FlAsH-ID preserved wild-type biology where a RUNX3–TurboID fusion broke protein function.
* **Nanoscale & Rapid Photoproximity Labeling:**
  * **µMap & Split-intein µMap (*Nat Chem Biol* & *Nat Commun*, Aug 2026):** Light-activated iridium photocatalysts using short-range Dexter energy transfer (including traceless split-intein attachment) have enabled high-precision mapping of tricky chromatin/transcription factor complexes like c-Myc and ERα in specific cellular/drug states.
  * **Lantern (Zou Lab, *Nat Chem Biol*, Sept 2026):** A genetically encoded photocatalyst evolved in mammalian cells that cuts protein and RNA proximity labeling down to **30 seconds**, capturing ultra-fast events like stress-granule assembly.
  * **STUPPIT (*PLOS Biology*, Nov 2025):** Combines PUP-IT and split-TurboID to selectively capture **bridging/intermediary proteins** connecting two proteins that don't bind each other directly.

*Verdict:* TurboID hasn't disappeared yet because FlexID and FlAsH-ID are brand-new (late 2026), but **FlexID** and **FlAsH-ID/µMap** are poised to replace TurboID wherever spatial precision, endogenous biotin noise, or tag size is limiting.

### 2. Challenging Co-IP / AP-MS: In-Cell Structural & Lysis-Free Interactomics
Co-IP/AP-MS requires breaking open cells, which destroys weak, transient, membrane-embedded, or phase-separated complexes, creates post-lysis artifacts, and cannot tell direct binding partners apart from indirect complex members. In 2026, two MS-based paradigms pushed hard against co-IP:

* **In-Cell & Tissue Cross-Linking Mass Spectrometry (XL-MS) + AI:**
  * **Intact-Cell Structural XL-MS (*Nature Microbiology*, July 2026):** Researchers applied specialized in-cell XL-MS integrated with modified AlphaFold modeling inside intact virus-infected human cells, resolving direct host–virus contact interfaces inside fragile phase-separated structures (paraspeckles) that shatter during co-IP lysis.
  * **DUC-XL-MS & BLIS-XL-MS (*bioRxiv*, Sept 2026):** Combining gentle fractionation or tissue sectioning with proteome-wide XL-MS yielded **18,074 direct, residue-resolved PPIs** in human cells (9× larger than prior XL-MS datasets) and enabled *in situ* structural interactome remodeling directly in mouse brain tissue—something co-IP cannot provide at proteome scale with interface-level resolution.
* **High-Throughput Co-Fractionation MS (CF-MS):** As highlighted in a March 2026 *Molecular Omics* review (Frommelt, Aebersold & Fossati), ultra-fast DIA-MS acquisition has turned untagged, native co-fractionation into a scalable way to profile endogenous complex dynamics across many conditions without antibodies or tags.

*Verdict:* Co-IP remains much cheaper and easier for confirming a specific pair of proteins with good antibodies, so it isn't dead—but for unbiased complex discovery and native in-cell interfaces, **in-cell XL-MS + AI** and **DIA co-fractionation** are increasingly overtaking AP-MS.

### 3. Challenging Y2H: Proteome-Scale Structural AI & Pooled NGS Screens
Y2H struggles with artificial nuclear localization, lack of mammalian post-translational modifications (PTMs), and membrane-protein blindness. Two major alternatives gained traction by 2026:

* **Proteome-Scale AI Co-Folding & Interface Screening:** Following the landmark *Science* (late 2025) study pairing deep metagenomic alignments (`omicMSA`) with `RoseTTAFold2-PPI` and AlphaFold to screen the human interactome, 2026 brought specialized PPI scoring and co-folding models (e.g., **PPIscreenML** in *eLife*, **IsoDDE** for difficult/induced-fit interfaces). Many labs now run *in silico* co-folding screens *before* touching the bench.
  * *Why AI hasn't superseded experimental screens yet:* 2026 evaluations show proteome-wide structural prediction still recovers only ~12–32% of true human PPIs at high precision and struggles with disordered motifs, PTM-dependent binding, and host–pathogen interactions.
* **Cell-Agnostic Sequencing-Based Screens:** Methods like **SoPPIs** (*Nucleic Acids Research*, July 2026) replace Y2H's yeast-specific transcriptional readout with split-Cre/loxP DNA recombination read out by NGS—allowing all-by-all pooled PPI screening directly inside prokaryotic or eukaryotic/mammalian cells. Meanwhile, Y2H itself adapted into **semi-quantitative Y2H (sqY2H)** in 2026 to score how thousands of missense variants weaken or break binary interactions.

---

### Bottom Line for 2026
Nothing made Y2H, co-IP, or TurboID universally obsolete. Instead, the toolkit upgraded from **"one tag / one pulldown fits all"** to an integrated pipeline:
1. **Hypothesis generation:** AI structural screening (`AlphaFold 3` / `RF2-PPI`) instead of blind Y2H cDNA screens.
2. **High-precision live-cell discovery:** **FlexID**, **FlAsH-ID**, or **µMap** instead of BioID/TurboID when spatial specificity, endogenous biotin, or tag size matters.
3. **Native complex & interface mapping:** **In-cell XL-MS + AI** instead of co-IP when preserving fragile compartments and knowing *which residues touch* matters.
4. **Targeted validation:** Classic **co-IP** (and quantitative **sqY2H** / **NanoBRET** / **NanoBiT**) still serves as the fast, accessible confirmation step.
