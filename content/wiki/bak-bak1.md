# BAK1 (BAK)

BAK (BCL-2 homologous antagonist/killer) is a mitochondrial outer-membrane protein that forms the apoptotic pore together with [BAX](bax.md).

## Function

BAK is anchored permanently in the mitochondrial outer membrane in an inactive conformation. Stress-activated BH3-only proteins — BID and BIM in particular — expose the helix that lets BAK oligomerize; the BAK–BAX oligomers permeabilize the outer membrane, releasing cytochrome c into the cytosol and committing the cell to caspase-dependent death. Anti-apoptotic family members ([BCL-2](bcl-2.md), [BCL-xL](bcl-xl-bcl2l1.md), [MCL-1](mcl-1.md)) hold BAK shut, and the MCL-1–BAK pair is a common resistance axis in cancer therapy.

## In cancer

Cells lacking both BAX and BAK resist mitochondrial apoptosis almost completely, which makes the pair the execution gate of the intrinsic pathway. Tumors rarely delete BAK outright; they more often raise the anti-apoptotic proteins that keep it shut, and BH3-mimetic drugs work by prying BAK and BAX free. Viruses encode BAK-binding inhibitors, which is evidence of how central the gate is to the host–pathogen conflict.
