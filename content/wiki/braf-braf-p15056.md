# BRAF

BRAF is a serine/threonine kinase immediately downstream of [KRAS](kras-kras-p01116.md) in the MAPK cascade, and the carrier of the single most common activating mutation in melanoma.

## Function

BRAF is one of three RAF kinases. Activated RAS recruits BRAF to the membrane, where it dimerizes and phosphorylates [MEK](map2k1-map2k1-q02750.md), which phosphorylates ERK; ERK then drives the transcriptional program of proliferation. The kinase sits at the cascade's second tier, close enough to the membrane relay to be RAS-dependent, far enough downstream to be druggable with ATP-competitive inhibitors.

## In cancer

The V600E mutation — a single glutamate-for-valine substitution in the activation loop — occurs in about half of melanomas, most hairy cell leukemias, and a large share of papillary thyroid cancers. V600E activates the kinase without RAS, and the mutant protein can signal as a monomer, which is why selective BRAF inhibitors (vemurafenib, dabrafenib) work in V600E melanoma. Two resistance routes shape therapy: BRAF-wild-type tumors dimerize and escape monomer-selective inhibition, and treated tumors frequently reactivate upstream signaling, which is why BRAF inhibitors are combined with [MEK](map2k1-map2k1-q02750.md) inhibitors in practice. In RAS-mutant tumors, BRAF inhibitors are harmful — the drug drives BRAF–CRAF heterodimerization and accelerates the pathway.
