# IL-6

IL-6 (interleukin-6) is a pleiotropic cytokine that drives the acute-phase inflammatory response and, when produced chronically, sustains inflammation-associated cancer and aging.

## Function

IL-6 is secreted by macrophages, T cells, and stromal cells in response to infection and tissue damage. It signals through a receptor complex that activates JAK kinases and [STAT3](jak2-o60674.md) — the classical signaling mode in immune cells — and through a second mode (trans-signaling through soluble IL-6 receptor) that reaches cells lacking the membrane receptor, including endothelium and fibroblasts. Its acute-phase outputs are fever induction, hepatocyte production of CRP and fibrinogen, B-cell maturation, and Th17 differentiation.

## In cancer and aging

Chronic IL-6 production is a fixture of the tumor microenvironment: stromal and immune cells in inflamed tissue produce IL-6, STAT3 activation in premalignant cells promotes survival and immune evasion, and the feedback loop between inflammation and malignancy is self-sustaining. Senescent cells secrete IL-6 as a core component of the [SASP](cellular-senescence.md), which makes IL-6 one of the molecular links between [senescence](judith-campisi.md), chronic inflammation, and late-life cancer risk. Tocilizumab, an IL-6 receptor antibody, is approved for rheumatoid arthritis and is being studied in cytokine-release syndrome.
