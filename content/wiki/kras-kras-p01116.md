# KRAS

KRAS is a small GTP-binding protein that relays signals from growth-factor receptors to the kinase cascades that drive proliferation. It is among the most frequently activated oncogenes in human cancer.

## Function

KRAS is bound to the inner face of the plasma membrane. A growth-factor receptor such as [EGFR](egfr-erbb1-p00533.md) activates it by catalyzing GDP-to-GTP exchange; KRAS-GTP then recruits and activates RAF, starting the MAPK cascade toward [MEK](map2k1-map2k1-q02750.md) and ERK, alongside [PI3K](pik3ca-pik3ca-p42336.md) and other effectors. KRAS hydrolyzes its own bound GTP to switch off; GAP proteins accelerate this hydrolysis, and growth-factor receptors activate KRAS by promoting GDP-to-GTP exchange.

## In cancer

Point mutations at codons 12, 13, and 61 destroy the GTPase activity, and the protein then signals continuously. KRAS mutations occur in roughly 40% of colorectal cancers, 90% of pancreatic cancers, and a quarter of lung adenocarcinomas. A KRAS-mutant tumor signals downstream of the receptor no matter what the receptor does, which is why anti-EGFR antibodies help only KRAS-wild-type colorectal cancer - testing for KRAS before treatment is standard practice. KRAS was long considered undruggable because its nucleotide pocket binds GTP with picomolar affinity; covalent inhibitors of KRAS^G12C (sotorasib, adagrasib), approved from 2021, target the most common lung-cancer mutant.
