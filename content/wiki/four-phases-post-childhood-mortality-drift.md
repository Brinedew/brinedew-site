# The Four Phases of Post-Childhood Mortality Drift

The human mortality hazard rate reaches its lifetime minimum around age 10 (roughly 0.08 per 1000 per year), then rises through four qualitatively distinct phases — only one of which is actually driven by biological aging.

## Phase 1: The Juvenile Minimum (age 10–14)

Mortality hovers at 0.08–0.12 per 1000 — essentially flat. This is the true biological "warranty period": developmental defects have been resolved (the infant mortality hump), senescence has not yet begun, and the immune/enforcement systems of the cell collective are at peak performance. Both the Makeham term (A, age-independent external causes) and the Gompertz term (Bc^x, age-dependent senescent causes) are at their lifetime minima.

This is the baseline against which all subsequent mortality increase should be measured.

## Phase 2: The Accident Hump (age 14–25)

Mortality rises to ~0.75 per 1000 — roughly **10× the minimum**. But this rise is not senescence. It is almost entirely attributable to external causes: accidents, violence, risk-taking behavior, substance use. The Makeham term dominates.

In the cell-collective frame: this is a **governance failure**, not a system decay problem. The anti-defector machinery (senescence surveillance, DDR, apoptosis) is still functional at juvenile levels — it's being overwhelmed by behavioral defector loads that the evolutionary environment didn't pre-adapt for.

The magnitude of this hump varies dramatically by country and sex. In conflict zones, it is far larger. In high-income countries with good safety nets, it is suppressed.

## Phase 3: The Young Adult Plateau (age 25–32)

Mortality stabilizes around 0.73–0.77 per 1000. The log-mortality slope goes flat or even slightly negative (slope ≈ 0.0 to 0.04/year). The Makeham term and the nascent Gompertz term are in approximate equilibrium:

μ(x) ≈ A + Bc^x, with A ≈ 0.70 and Bc^25 ≈ 0.05

At age 30, only about 9% of deaths are attributable to senescent causes. The remaining 91% are still external/age-independent.

This plateau is often invisible in all-cause mortality plots because the absolute numbers are low, but it is clearly visible in log-mortality slopes. Some demographers have argued this plateau extends into the late 30s in exceptionally safe populations.

## Phase 4: True Gompertz Acceleration (age 32+)

The senescent component finally overtakes the external baseline. The log-mortality slope becomes consistently positive and linear at ~0.085/year, corresponding to a mortality doubling time of approximately 8 years.

At age 40, senescent causes become dominant (>50% of deaths). By age 60, >95% of deaths are senescent. The Makeham term becomes quantitatively negligible after 50.

The Gompertz slope is remarkably constant across human populations; only the intercept (initial mortality level) varies.

## Implications

### For the cell-collective frame:
- Phase 1 (10–14): The cell collective is at its most robust — surveillance, differentiation, and enforcement are all working at evolutionary baseline.
- Phase 2 (14–25): The collective faces a defector flood from external inputs (behavior, environment). The governance system is strained but not broken.
- Phase 3 (25–32): The collective stabilizes at a new equilibrium with a higher defector baseline.
- Phase 4 (32+): The governance system itself begins to decay — senescence surveillance fails, DDR becomes error-prone, cell loyalty erodes. The problem shifts from "too many defectors" to "the police are becoming defectors."

### For intervention strategy:
- Interventions targeting external causes (accident prevention, risk reduction) can only suppress mortality down to the Makeham floor — they cannot touch the Gompertz slope.
- Interventions targeting the Gompertz intercept (reducing baseline senescent mortality) shift the whole curve down but do not change the doubling time.
- Interventions targeting the Gompertz slope (slowing the rate of aging) change the fundamental trajectory — they are the only interventions that can "bend the curve" after 32.
- The 10-year gap between the juvenile minimum and the start of measurable senescent mortality (~10 to ~32) is a window during which damage is accumulating but mortality is dominated by external causes. This is the "stealth phase" of aging — the senescent load is building but masked by the accident hump and plateau.

## Data sources

This analysis uses stylized mortality rates from the Human Mortality Database (USA, female, ~2019) and the Gompertz–Makeham framework. Exact numbers vary by year, sex, and country, but the qualitative shape is universal across all human populations.
