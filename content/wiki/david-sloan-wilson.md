# David Sloan Wilson

![David Sloan Wilson](../Attachments/david-sloan-wilson.png)

**David Sloan Wilson** (born 1949) is an American evolutionary biologist and professor emeritus at Binghamton University, known for reviving [multilevel selection](multilevel-selection.md) theory and extending it to animal behavior, cultural evolution, and civic institutions.

## Multilevel selection and empirical work

In the 1970s, Wilson developed mathematical trait-group models demonstrating how selection between transient, localized interaction groups can outweigh selection against cooperators within each group. Together with philosopher Elliott Sober, he synthesized this perspective in *Unto Others: The Evolution and Psychology of Unselfish Behavior* (1998), summarizing the trade-off as: *"Selfishness beats altruism within groups. Altruistic groups beat selfish groups. Everything else is commentary."*[^1]

Outside theoretical modeling, Wilson studied behavioral ecology and phenotypic divergence in freshwater fish—working with Lee Alan Dugatkin and Beren Robinson on Trinidadian guppies (*Poecilia reticulata*) and polymorphic sunfish—connecting predation risk, cooperative predator inspection, and local resource competition to population differentiation.

## Debates with inclusive-fitness theorists

Wilson's rehabilitation of multilevel selection prompted a sustained debate with inclusive-fitness theorists, including Stuart West, Andy Gardner, and Ashleigh Griffin.[^2] Gene-centered critics contend that trait-group selection is mathematically equivalent to inclusive fitness ([Hamilton's rule](wd-hamilton.md)) and warn that invoking group selection obscures clarity and risks reviving naive group-adaptation fallacies.[^2] Wilson replies that mathematical inter-translatability does not imply causal equivalence: partitioning variance across levels highlights group-level selective pressures—especially in non-kin collectives and human institutions—that individual-level bookkeeping can mask.[^1][^4]

## Cultural evolution and applied prosociality

Wilson extended multilevel selection to human cultural systems, interpreting religious traditions in *Darwin's Cathedral* (2002) as group-level mechanisms that promote cooperation and constrain internal defection.[^3] He later applied these principles through the Binghamton Neighborhood Project and the Prosocial initiative—combining Elinor Ostrom's commons-governance rules with behavioral psychology—and summarized his evolutionary worldview in *This View of Life: Completing the Darwinian Revolution* (2019).[^4]
