# VEGFA (VEGF)

VEGF-A (vascular endothelial growth factor A) is the master signal for new blood vessel growth: the factor hypoxic tissues release to recruit a blood supply, and the factor tumors release to feed themselves.

## Function

VEGF-A is secreted by cells experiencing hypoxia — HIF-1α, which accumulates when oxygen is scarce, directly induces its transcription. It binds VEGFR2 on endothelial cells, driving their proliferation, migration, and survival, and increasing vessel permeability so that plasma proteins leak out and form a scaffold for new vessel growth. Physiologically this is wound healing, the menstrual cycle, and embryonic vascular development; pathologically it is the angiogenic switch by which an avascular tumor acquires a blood supply.

## In cancer

Tumors beyond a millimeter or two in diameter need induced vasculature, and VEGF-A is the dominant inducer. The tumor vasculature that VEGF produces is structurally abnormal — leaky, disorganized, poorly perfused — which both feeds the tumor and limits drug delivery. Anti-VEGF therapy (bevacizumab, the antibody; sorafenib and related receptor kinase inhibitors) is approved across several cancers, with the clinical lesson that blockade delays progression and extends survival modestly rather than eradicating disease, and with resistance arriving through alternative angiogenic factors. VEGF-A also participates in the [senescence-associated secretory phenotype](cellular-senescence.md).
