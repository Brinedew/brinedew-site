# Iconoplasm - Caretaker's Handbook

**Who are caretakers?**

Any Iconoplasm user can visit a gene page and become that gene's **caretaker**. 

**It's a 1-to-1 match:** each gene only gets to have one caretaker, and a caretaker can only care for one gene at a time. 

However, **it's not a commitment:** as a caretaker, you can switch genes anytime.

Caretakers have great control over the gene's character identity:
* Caretaker can modify the gene lore snippet (up to 10,000 characters long).
* Caretaker can modify gene visual tags that are used for image generation.
* Caretaker can grant their single transferable supervote to the candidate portrait of their liking.

Caretakers can help the Iconoplasm project in multiple ways:
* **Worldbuilding:** you can modify the gene's lore and appearance in a way that suits your creative narrative.
* **Quality control**: If the gene's portraits consistently come out to contradict their assigned value on the gene card, you can become a caretaker and correct the deviation.
* **Social coordination**: Groups of caretakers can coordinate to propose a better mapping for a clan or a gene family.

**How do I become a caretaker?**

1. Go to any gene page
2. Tap "Become this gene's caretaker" button under the gene card

**Who can become a caretaker?**
At the moment, anyone with a Discord account can become a caretaker. You don't need to be a member of Brinedew.bio discord server to be a caretaker. This might change in the future.

**Do I need to have a publishing record about this gene to become its caretaker?**

No, interest alone is enough. My hope is that biology students and field newcomers will first learn the biology of their chosen gene, familiarize themselves with foundational biological concepts through that character, and then use their gene as an anchor to explore other parts of molecular cell biology.

To get up to speed after becoming a caretaker, try to get familiar with basics:

- Your gene's protein products, their subcellular location and molecular function.
- Your gene's downstream effects in normal and pathological states.
- Your gene's expression pattern across tissues and developmental stages.
- What others genes are similar to yours in structure and function.
- What other genes interact with yours on both protein level and transcriptional regulation level.

**How long can I stay a caretaker of a single gene?**
There is no time limit - you can settle on your favorite gene and stay its caretaker for as long as you'd like. A vision for Iconoplasm is to eventually settle down to a state where every gene has had the same caretaker for decades at a time.

However, it's possible that you could be removed from the caretaker position against your wish. One example would be going silent for months at a time despite getting coordination requests from other caretakers. Other such removal policies might be implemented in the future in the interest of the project's continued flourishing.

**How many times can I change which gene I'm caretaking?**

While you can only caretake one gene at a time, there's no limit on how many times you can change which gene you're a caretaker for. 

A gene change frequency limit, currently at 1 change per 15 minutes, will get adjusted to be slower in the future. This way, the character roster might start a bit chaotic but will gradually anneal down to a stable state.

**What if I want to swap genes with another caretaker?**

Go to a gene page and click the "request swap" button in the gene toolbar. The caretaker for that gene will get a swap request notification and can either accept or reject it. You can only have one swap request active at a time.

**What happens when two caretakers can't compromise on a compatible narrative?**

For now, I will adjudicate the conflicts personally. In the future, a formal conflict resolution system might get implemented.

**Can the displayed lore snippet on a gene page conflict with canonical image on the same page?**

**I contribute work - how is it going to be used?**

I expect other people might use the character designs for downstream content:
- Adding character images to pathway diagrams
- Making mnemonic flashcard decks
- Featuring the characters in books, videos, and games
- Selling merch or figurines based on the character

**Please note:** As explained in Caretaker Terms, when you contribute to Iconoplasm, you dedicate your creative work (lore writing and curation) to public domain under the CC0 license where applicable. This means that anyone else can modify your Iconoplasm design, monetize it, and distribute it as they see fit, without needing to credit you.
### Generation tips

**I struggle to make the characters look the right mass**

The auto-tagger's default tendency is to add descriptor tags like "petite" or "sturdy" to the character's tag cloud. However, this can fail to intuitively translate character mass to the viewer. The problem becomes bigger at the extremes like 10kg or 500kg characters.

In my testing, I found three approaches to make the weight readable from an image:

1. Specify a head-to-body ratio as a separate character tag

| Weight | How many heads tall |
| ------ | ------------------- |
| 5 kg   | 2                   |
| 10 kg  | 3                   |
| 20 kg  | 4                   |
| 30 kg  | 5                   |
| 40 kg  | 6                   |
| 60 kg  | 7                   |
| 80 kg  | 8                   |
| 160 kg | 9                   |
| 320 kg | 10                  |
2. "Banana for scale" - describe how big or small the surrounding props are. Character who "dwarfed by the height of the doorframe" comes off as smaller mass compared to the character who "crouches to fit into doorframe".
3. Compare the physique to known characters: "hulk physique", "goblin proportions", ""


**I struggle to make the character look their age**

In my experience, eye-to-head ratio is great at conveying maturity at a glance.
