# Parliament of genes

The **"parliament of genes"** is an evolutionary metaphor proposed by biologist Egbert Leigh in 1971 to explain why an organism's body acts as a cooperative, unified whole rather than a chaotic free-for-all of competing genetic parts
***
The Core Concept

- **The Metaphor:** Imagine an organism’s genome as a vast parliament where individual genes have their own localized "interests" or effects. [[1](https://www.statedclearly.com/videos/genetic-conflict-and-the-parliament-of-genes/)]

- **Selfish Genetic Elements ("Cabals"):** Occasionally, a mutation creates a "selfish" genetic element (such as a meiotic driver or transposon) that tries to distort inheritance or manipulate the organism's traits selfishly for its own transmission at a cost to the rest. [[1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-13169-3), [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35690638/), [3](https://www.statedclearly.com/videos/genetic-conflict-and-the-parliament-of-genes/)]

- **The Parliamentary Majority:** Because the vast majority of the genome shares a common interest in the overall health, survival, and fair reproduction of the entire organism, the "majority" of genes are naturally selected to evolve **suppressor elements** (policing genes) that shut down or outvote the selfish rebels. [[1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-13169-3), [2](https://www.statedclearly.com/videos/genetic-conflict-and-the-parliament-of-genes/)]

- **Individual Fitness:** As a result, internal genetic conflicts are typically suppressed before they can harm the whole organism, preserving the illusion and reality of **individual fitness maximization**. [[1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-13169-3), [2](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/adaptation-is-maintained-by-the-parliament-of-genes)]
