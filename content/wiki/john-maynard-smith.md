# John Maynard Smith

![John Maynard Smith](../Attachments/john-maynard-smith.jpg)

John Maynard Smith (1920–2004) was a British evolutionary biologist, professor at the University of Sussex, celebrated for introducing game theory to evolutionary biology, clarifying the paradox of the evolution of sex, and co-authoring the [major evolutionary transitions](evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality.md) framework.

## Biography and honors

Maynard Smith initially studied engineering at **Trinity College, Cambridge**, and worked as an aeronautical engineer during World War II before switching careers to study zoology and genetics at **University College London (UCL)** under J. B. S. Haldane. After lecturing at UCL, he became founding dean and professor of biology at the University of Sussex.

He received the Royal Society's Royal Medal, the Kyoto Prize, and in **1999** shared the **Crafoord Prize in Biosciences** jointly with **George C. Williams** and **Ernst Mayr** for foundational contributions to evolutionary theory.

## Evolutionary game theory and sex ratios

In the 1970s (with George Price), Maynard Smith imported mathematical game theory into evolution via the **evolutionarily stable strategy (ESS)** — a strategy such that, once prevalent in a population, no rare mutant strategy can invade. His book *Evolution and the Theory of Games* (1982) recast animal contests, honest signaling, and Fisherian **sex-ratio equilibrium** as ESS problems: equal parental investment in sons and daughters emerges whenever deviating from 1:1 grants an immediate reproductive payoff to the rarer sex, without requiring species-level coordination.

Earlier, in his 1971 essay and ***The Evolution of Sex* (1978)**, Maynard Smith crystallized the **"twofold cost of males"** (or cost of meiosis): an asexual parthenogenetic female passes all of her genes to every offspring and produces only daughters, whereas a sexual female invests half her reproductive output into sons that cannot bear offspring themselves. Explaining how ecological and genomic advantages—such as [Red Queen](red-queen-hypothesis.md) pathogen evasion or mutational purging—overcome that short-term twofold penalty became one of evolutionary biology's central challenges.

## Major transitions

With [Eörs Szathmáry](eors-szathmary.md), Maynard Smith co-authored *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (1995), organizing life's history around shifts in how hereditary information and reproductive autonomy pass from independent lower-level replicators into cooperative higher-level individuals—a framework directly informing [multicellular cooperation](multicellular-cooperation.md) and somatic defection in cancer.
