# UBE3A

UBE3A (E6-associated protein) is the E3 ubiquitin ligase whose loss causes Angelman syndrome and whose silencing in neurons is the textbook case of genomic imprinting in humans.

## Function

UBE3A tags substrate proteins with ubiquitin for proteasomal destruction. Its best-characterized substrate relationship runs in reverse: HPV E6 protein recruits UBE3A to [p53](p53-tp53.md), and the ligase ubiquitinates p53 for degradation — the mechanism by which HPV-positive tumors delete their p53 response without mutating the gene. UBE3A's normal neuronal substrates include synaptic proteins, and its dosage in the brain shapes synaptic development.

## Genomic imprinting

In most tissues both parental copies of UBE3A are active. In neurons the maternal copy alone is active: the paternal allele is silenced by the UBE3A-ATS long noncoding RNA. This parent-of-origin expression is genomic imprinting, and it makes the gene's phenotype haploid in the brain. Loss of the maternal allele causes Angelman syndrome (severe developmental delay, seizures, ataxic gait); duplication of the maternal allele causes a subset of autism cases. The same dosage sensitivity explains why UBE3A is an autism candidate gene.

## In cancer

In HPV-driven cancers, E6 hijacks UBE3A to destroy [p53](p53-tp53.md); in HPV-negative cancers UBE3A is more often overexpressed or amplified, acting as an oncogene through degradation of tumor-suppressive substrates.
