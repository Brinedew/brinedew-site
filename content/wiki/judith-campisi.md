# Judith Campisi

![Judith Campisi](../Attachments/judith-campisi.jpg)

Judith Campisi (1948–2024) was an American cell and molecular biologist renowned for transforming [cellular senescence](cellular-senescence.md) from an *in vitro* curiosity into a central mechanism connecting cancer suppression and aging. She was a professor at the Buck Institute for Research on Aging and a senior scientist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

## Career and honors

Campisi received her PhD in biochemistry from the State University of New York at Stony Brook and trained as a postdoctoral fellow at Harvard Medical School. After faculty positions at Boston University Medical School, she was recruited to Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory in **1990** by [Mina Bissell](mina-bissell.md) and joined the Buck Institute in 2002, leading a laboratory until her death in January 2024. She also co-founded Unity Biotechnology to translate senescence-clearing therapies.

For bridging tumor suppression and geroscience, she was elected to the **National Academy of Sciences**, received the **2010 Ipsen Foundation Longevity Prize**, and was the inaugural recipient of the **2015 Olav Thon Foundation Prize** in Natural Sciences and Medicine.

## Antagonistic pleiotropy of senescence and the SASP

Campisi argued across an influential review series—including *"Senescent cells, tumor suppression, and organismal aging: good citizens, bad neighbors"* (*Cell*, 2005) and *"Aging, Cellular Senescence, and Cancer"* (*Annual Review of Physiology*, 2013)—that cellular senescence exemplifies evolutionary [antagonistic pleiotropy](antagonistic-pleiotropy-theory.md):

- **Early-life benefit.** Irreversible proliferative arrest triggered by telomere attrition, DNA damage, or oncogene activation prevents damaged cells from dividing into malignant tumors, preserving organismal integrity during peak reproduction.
- **Late-life cost and the SASP.** As arrested cells persist and accumulate in aged tissues, they corrupt surrounding tissue homeostasis via paracrine signaling. In a pivotal *PNAS* study (Krtolica et al., 2001), Campisi's group showed that senescent human fibroblasts stimulate premalignant and malignant epithelial neighbors to form tumors *in vivo*. Working with Jean-Philippe Coppé (*PLoS Biology*, 2008; *Annual Review of Pathology*, 2010), she defined and characterized this secretome—inflammatory cytokines (including [IL-6](il-6.md), [IL-8](il-8-cxcl8.md)), growth factors, and matrix proteases—as the **senescence-associated secretory phenotype ([SASP](sasp.md))**, establishing the mechanistic "dark side of tumor suppression."

Campisi's synthesis directly informs models in which anti-neoplastic defenses trade off against late-life tissue integrity, including [senolytics](senolytics.md) and the [[tumor-suppressor-theory-of-aging|tumor suppressor theory of aging]].
