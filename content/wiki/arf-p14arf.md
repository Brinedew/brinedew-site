# p14ARF

p14^ARF, encoded by the CDKN2A locus, is a tumor suppressor that guards [p53](p53-tp53.md) by sequestering its destroyer, [MDM2](mdm2.md), in the nucleolus.

## Function

ARF responds to hyperproliferative signals — oncogene activation, E2F overactivity — rather than to DNA damage. Activated ARF binds MDM2 in the nucleolus, holding it away from p53; p53 stabilizes and accumulates, and the cell arrests or dies. The protein therefore completes a surveillance loop with a different trigger from the [ATM](atm.md) pathway: DNA damage signals through ATM to p53, while oncogene activation signals through ARF to p53, so a cell faces p53-mediated arrest whether its genome is damaged or its growth circuitry is hijacked. The mouse homolog, p19^ARF, serves the same role with different biochemistry.

## In cancer

ARF shares the CDKN2A locus with [p16](p16ink4a-cdkn2a.md), encoded in an alternative reading frame of the same DNA — a rare genetic arrangement that puts two tumor suppressors in the space of one gene. Deletion of the locus removes both at once, which is why CDKN2A loss is so frequent in cancer. ARF promoter methylation occurs in colon and gastric tumors with intact p16, and ARF loss cooperates with MYC overexpression in lymphoma models.
