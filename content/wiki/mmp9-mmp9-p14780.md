# MMP9

MMP9 (matrix metalloproteinase 9, gelatinase B) is a secreted enzyme that degrades collagen IV and gelatin, the structural components of basement membranes.

## Function

MMP9 is secreted as a zymogen by neutrophils, macrophages, and stromal cells and activated by proteolytic cleavage at the membrane or in the extracellular space. Its substrates are basement-membrane collagens and denatured collagen (gelatin), so its physiological roles involve breaking through barriers: wound healing, bone remodeling, immune-cell diapedesis, and endometrial cycling. The enzyme is regulated at three levels - transcription (induced by inflammatory cytokines such as [TNF](tnf-tnf-p01375.md) and [IL-6](il-6.md)), zymogen activation, and inhibition by TIMP tissue inhibitors - and its activity therefore reflects the balance between inflammatory demand and tissue integrity.

## In cancer

Tumors recruit MMP9 from stromal cells and neutrophils more than they produce it themselves. The enzyme clears basement membrane for invasion, releases matrix-bound growth factors ([VEGF](vegf-vegfa.md) among them), and generates collagen fragments that promote angiogenesis and motility. MMP9 expression correlates with metastasis across tumor types. The broad-spectrum MMP inhibitor trials of the 1990s failed - wrong substrates, wrong stage of disease - and the lesson they left is that matrix proteolysis works as a system, with redundancy among family members.
