# IL-10

IL-10 (interleukin-10) is the principal anti-inflammatory cytokine: its job is to limit immune activation and tissue damage once a response has done its work.

## Function

IL-10 is produced by regulatory T cells, macrophages, and other immune cells after activation. It binds the IL-10 receptor on macrophages and dendritic cells and suppresses their production of pro-inflammatory cytokines ([TNF](tnf-tnf-p01375.md), [IL-6](il-6.md), IL-12), their antigen-presentation capacity, and their costimulatory molecule expression — a broad dampening of the innate immune response. The signal runs through JAK1–STAT3, the same transcription factor [pro-inflammatory cytokines activate](jak2-o60674.md), with opposite transcriptional output because of the target genes available in each cell type.

## In cancer

Tumors and tumor-associated macrophages exploit IL-10's brake. IL-10-rich tumor microenvironments suppress cytotoxic T-cell and NK-cell function, reducing the immune pressure on the tumor — one route of the [immune escape](immune-surveillance.md) that immunoediting selects for. IL-10 overexpression correlates with poor prognosis in several cancers. Therapeutically the cytokine cuts both ways: pegilodecakin (PEG-IL-10) was tested for its ability to reprogram exhausted T cells, while IL-10 blockade is being studied to release the brake on antitumor immunity.
