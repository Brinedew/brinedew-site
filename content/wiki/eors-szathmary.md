# Eörs Szathmáry

![Eörs Szathmáry](../Attachments/eors-szathmary.jpg)

Eörs Szathmáry (born 1959) is a Hungarian theoretical evolutionary biologist, professor at Eötvös Loránd University in Budapest, and director of the Parmenides Center for the Conceptual Foundations of Science in Munich. He is best known for co-authoring the [major evolutionary transitions](evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality.md) framework with [John Maynard Smith](john-maynard-smith.md) and for theoretical work on the origin of life and the genetic code.

## The Hungarian school of theoretical biology and Gánti's chemoton

Szathmáry grew up intellectually within Hungary's quantitative theoretical-biology tradition, notably as a student and close collaborator of **Tibor Gánti**, originator of the **chemoton** model of minimal cellular life. Gánti's chemoton integrates three stoichiometrically coupled chemical subsystems — an autocatalytic metabolic cycle, a template-copying genetic polymer, and a growing boundary membrane — into one indivisible living unit.

Working directly from this lineage, Szathmáry formalized early compartment models (*stochastic corrector* dynamics) showing how bounding compartments resolve selfish-replicator parasites before specialized cellular chromosomes exist: proto-cells dividing randomly survive only if fast, selfish replicators cannot overrun essential cooperative templates across compartment generations. That parasite-containment logic carried straight into his later major-transitions theory.

## Major transitions

With Maynard Smith, Szathmáry co-authored *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (1995) and *The Origins of Life: From the Birth of Life to the Origin of Language* (1999). Their sequence analyzes how previously independent units lose autonomy to compose higher-level individuals — from molecular replicators to chromosomes, prokaryotes to eukaryotes, single cells to multicellular organisms, and solitary organisms to eusocial societies — always emphasizing the policing and bottleneck constraints that hold internal defection in check.

## Coding Coenzyme Handles and the origin of the genetic code

In his 1993 *PNAS* paper *"Coding coenzyme handles: a hypothesis for the origin of the genetic code"*, Szathmáry addressed how specific triplet–amino-acid assignments could evolve before coded ribosomal protein synthesis existed. Rather than assuming pure chance or requiring a full protein translation apparatus first, the CCH hypothesis proposes that in an RNA World, short oligonucleotide "handles" (ancestral anticodons) were charged with amino acids and acted directly as catalytically useful coenzymes assisting ribozyme chemistry. Stereochemical and functional binding between oligonucleotide handles, amino acid side chains, and ribozyme pockets provided early selection for specific assignments, bridging metabolic utility and symbolic coding.

Szathmáry has also contributed to Darwinian neurodynamics, evolutionary linguistics, and served as Director General of the Centre for Ecological Research in Hungary (2019–2021).
