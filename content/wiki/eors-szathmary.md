# Eörs Szathmáry

![Eörs Szathmáry](../Attachments/eors-szathmary.jpg)

**Eörs Szathmáry** (born 1959) is a Hungarian theoretical evolutionary biologist, professor at Eötvös Loránd University in Budapest, and director of the Parmenides Center for the Conceptual Foundations of Science in Munich. He is best known for co-authoring the [major evolutionary transitions](evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality.md) framework with [John Maynard Smith](john-maynard-smith.md) and for theoretical work on the origin of life and the genetic code.

## Gánti's chemoton and stochastic corrector models

Szathmáry trained within Hungary's theoretical biology school as a student and collaborator of Tibor Gánti, creator of the **chemoton** model of minimal cellular life. The chemoton couples three chemical subsystems—an autocatalytic metabolic network, a template-copying genetic polymer, and a boundary membrane—into an integrated reproducing entity.

Building on Gánti's framework, Szathmáry developed compartment models (*stochastic corrector* dynamics) explaining how protocells survive selfish replicators before chromosomes evolve: when templates assort stochastically across dividing compartments, fast-replicating parasites destroy host compartments that lose essential cooperative enzymes, selecting for balanced multi-gene complements. That emphasis on suppressing internal defection carried directly into his transitions work.[^2]

## Major transitions

With Maynard Smith, Szathmáry co-authored *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (1995) and *The Origins of Life* (1999).[^2][^3] Their synthesis traces how independent units integrate into higher-level Darwinian individuals—from protocells and chromosomes through eukaryotes, multicellular organisms, and eusocial colonies—emphasizing both new inheritance systems and conflict-suppression barriers that prevent lower-level cheating.[^2]

## Origin of the genetic code

Szathmáry proposed the **coding coenzyme handles** hypothesis to explain how early triplet–amino-acid assignments could arise before ribosomal translation evolved.[^1] Under this RNA-world scenario, short oligonucleotides acting as proto-anticodons were charged with amino acids and served directly as catalytic coenzymes for ribozymes; specific stereochemical binding between handles, amino-acid side chains, and ribozyme active sites created natural selection for coded specificity prior to protein synthesis.[^1]

Szathmáry has also worked on Darwinian neurodynamics and evolutionary linguistics, and served as Director General of the Centre for Ecological Research in Hungary (2019–2021).
