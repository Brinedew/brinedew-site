# SHH (Sonic Hedgehog)

SHH (Sonic hedgehog) is a secreted morphogen that organizes embryonic patterning and maintains adult stem-cell compartments; its pathway reappears as a cancer pathway when reactivated in adult tissue.

## Function

SHH is lipid- and cholesterol-modified, which shapes it into multimeric particles that spread through tissue as a gradient. Cells receiving SHH through the PTCH1 receptor release SMO from inhibition, and GLI transcription factors (GLI1/2 as activators, GLI3 as repressor) convert the graded signal into positional identity - the mechanism by which the embryonic neural tube, limb buds, and gut are patterned. In adult tissue, SHH signaling maintains stem cells in skin, gut, and brain.

## In cancer

Reactivated SHH signaling causes two distinct cancer classes. Loss-of-function mutations in PTCH1 (a negative regulator of the pathway) cause basal cell carcinoma and medulloblastoma - the receptor's loss mimics ligand presence, and the pathway runs constitutively; this is the "ligand-independent" mode, and SMO inhibitors (vismodegib, sonidegib) work here. In pancreatic, prostate, and lung cancers, tumor and stromal cells produce SHH ligand itself, recruiting a fibrotic, immunosuppressive stroma - the "ligand-dependent" mode, which SMO inhibitors failed to treat and which requires targeting the GLI transcription factors downstream.

## Notes

- Human gene symbol: SHH; UniProt Q15465; 462 amino acids (precursor); Hedgehog family.
- Named for the mutant-wing phenotype in *Drosophila*; the mammalian homologs are named for hedgehog species.
