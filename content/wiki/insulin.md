# Insulin

Insulin is the pancreatic hormone that signals nutrient abundance: it commands tissues to absorb glucose, store energy, and grow, and its signaling pathway is the evolutionary ancestor of the aging-control systems studied in worms and flies.

## Function

Insulin is secreted by pancreatic β-cells when blood glucose rises after a meal. It binds the insulin receptor, a tyrosine kinase that recruits IRS proteins and activates two main routes: the [PI3K](pik3ca-pik3ca-p42336.md)–[AKT](akt2-akt2-p31751.md) arm, which moves GLUT4 transporters into muscle and fat cell membranes and drives glycogen and lipid storage; and the [MAPK](map2k1-map2k1-q02750.md) arm, which drives growth. Between meals, insulin falls and the body mobilizes stores — the hormone's absence is as physiologically important as its presence.

## In aging and cancer

Insulin signaling sits at the top of an evolutionarily conserved aging axis: reduced insulin/IGF signaling extends lifespan in *C. elegans* (daf-2), *Drosophila*, and mice, and the pathway's downstream effectors — [FOXO transcription factors](foxo3-foxo3-o43524.md), [mTOR](mtor-mtor-p42345.md) — are the molecular machinery of that extension. Chronic insulin elevation (insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome) raises IGF-1 signaling, mitotic drive, and cancer risk. Hyperinsulinemia is a recognized tumor-promoting condition, particularly for colorectal and endometrial cancer.
