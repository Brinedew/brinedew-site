# Mel Greaves

![Mel Greaves](../Attachments/mel-greaves.jpg)

**Sir Melvyn ("Mel") Greaves** (born 1941) is a British cancer biologist and professor at the Institute of Cancer Research in London, known for integrating Darwinian [clonal evolution](clonal-evolution.md) and [cancer ecology](cancer-ecology.md) with experimental hematology, particularly in childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).

## Career and honors

Greaves built his research career in London immunology and leukemia biology, founding and directing the Centre for Evolution and Cancer at the Institute of Cancer Research. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2003, received the Royal Society's Royal Medal in 2017, and was appointed Knight Bachelor in 2019 for services to childhood leukemia research.

## Evolutionary oncology and childhood leukemia

In *Cancer: The Evolutionary Legacy* (2000), Greaves analyzed malignancy as a Darwinian process inside tissues, attributing human vulnerability both to modern environmental exposures and to evolutionary trade-offs shaped over human life history.[^1]

Empirically, Greaves reconstructed the natural history of pediatric ALL, especially cases bearing the *ETV6-RUNX1* translocation. Twin studies and neonatal blood-spot analyses showed that *ETV6-RUNX1* arises prenatally *in utero*, establishing a covert pre-leukemic clone, whereas overt leukemia requires subsequent cooperating genetic hits after birth. His **delayed infection hypothesis** proposes that low microbial exposure in early infancy leaves immune networks insufficiently trained, so later common infections trigger proliferative and inflammatory stress that promotes secondary mutations in pre-leukemic cells.

Single-cell clonal architecture analyses led by Greaves demonstrated that *ETV6-RUNX1* ALL progresses through **genetic variegation**—branching trees of coexisting subclones carrying distinct secondary lesions prior to therapy—meaning relapse frequently emerges from minor pre-existing subclones rather than the dominant clone at diagnosis.[^2]
