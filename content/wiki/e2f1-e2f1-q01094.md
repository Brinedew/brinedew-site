# E2F1

E2F1 is a transcription factor that activates the S-phase gene program — the set of genes a cell needs to replicate its DNA — and doubles as a sensor of unsolicited proliferation.

## Function

E2F1 drives transcription of genes for DNA synthesis: dihydrofolate reductase, thymidine kinase, cyclin E, and [APAF1](apaf1.md) among them. Its activity is gated by [RB](rb-rb1.md), which binds E2F1 at promoters and keeps them silent until cyclin–CDK phosphorylation releases it at the G1/S transition. The protein carries its own brake: when a cell is pushed into cycle without proper growth signals, E2F1 activates [p14ARF](arf-p14arf.md), which stabilizes [p53](p53-tp53.md) and triggers arrest or death. E2F1 also induces its own promoter, creating a positive feedback that makes the S-phase commitment switch-like.

## In cancer

Because E2F1 is the endpoint of the RB pathway, every tumor that disables RB — by mutation, by HPV E7, or by cyclin amplification — unleashes E2F1, and S-phase genes are transcribed on demand. The ARF loop is the reason unsolicited E2F1 activity is dangerous for the tumor: a proliferating cell must also lose p53 or ARF, or the E2F1 pulse kills it. Viral oncogenes target both gates together, which is how HPV converts infection to cancer.
