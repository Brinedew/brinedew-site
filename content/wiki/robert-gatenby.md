# Robert Gatenby

![Robert Gatenby](../Attachments/robert-gatenby.jpg)

Robert A. Gatenby is an American physician-scientist, radiologist, and professor at the Moffitt Cancer Center in Tampa, Florida, known for founding [adaptive therapy](adaptive-therapy.md) and for developing an evolutionary and [ecological framework](cancer-ecology.md) for tumorigenesis and drug resistance.

## Warburg effect and metabolic tumor ecology

Before moving to clinical dosing protocols, Gatenby (most notably with Robert J. Gillies in *Nature Reviews Cancer*, 2004) reframed one of oncology's oldest biochemical puzzles — the **Warburg effect** (aerobic glycolysis even when oxygen is present) — as an evolutionary adaptation to fluctuating glucose and oxygen gradients in avascular premalignant lesions.

In Gatenby and Gillies's ecological model, cells growing away from blood vessels experience intermittent hypoxia and select for upregulated constitutive glycolysis. That metabolic switch carries an ecological payoff: exporting lactic acid and protons ($H^+$) lowers the extracellular pH of the surrounding tissue. Normal somatic cells undergo acid-induced death and matrix metalloproteinases degrade the extracellular matrix, whereas acid-adapted tumor clones survive and expand into the newly cleared territory — a process Gatenby termed **acid-mediated invasion**. Glucose competition and pH niche construction thus turn metabolism from an intracellular housekeeping defect into an ecological weapon against host tissue.

## Evolutionary game theory and adaptive therapy

Gatenby's group paired Lotka–Volterra resource-competition models with evolutionary game theory (collaborating closely with evolutionary ecologist Joel Brown and mathematician Kateřina Staňková) to argue that maximum-tolerated-dose (MTD) chemotherapy creates an avoidable evolutionary trap: wiping out sensitive clones relieves resource competition and triggers the rapid expansion of resistant lineages (*competitive release*). In their 2009 *Cancer Research* proposal and subsequent Stackelberg game formulations (Gatenby & Brown, 2020), the clinician acts as a strategic leader modulating drug pressure so that fitter sensitive cells remain abundant enough to suppress resistant phenotypes.

The strategy received its landmark clinical test in a 2017 *Nature Communications* pilot study of abiraterone in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (Zhang et al., 2017), extending median time to progression to at least 27 months while cutting average cumulative drug dose to roughly 47% of standard continuous therapy.

At Moffitt, Gatenby founded the Integrated Mathematical Oncology program, pairing mathematicians and experimentalists, and co-directs the Center of Excellence for Evolutionary Therapy.
