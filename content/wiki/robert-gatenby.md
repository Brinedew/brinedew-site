# Robert Gatenby

![Robert Gatenby](../Attachments/robert-gatenby.jpg)

**Robert A. Gatenby** is an American physician-scientist, radiologist, and professor at the Moffitt Cancer Center in Tampa, Florida, known for introducing [adaptive therapy](adaptive-therapy.md) and applying [evolutionary ecology](cancer-ecology.md) to tumorigenesis and drug resistance.[^1][^2]

## Warburg effect and metabolic tumor ecology

In collaboration with Robert J. Gillies, Gatenby reframed the **Warburg effect**—aerobic glycolysis in cancer cells despite the presence of oxygen—as an evolutionary adaptation to fluctuating glucose and oxygen gradients in avascular premalignant lesions.[^1]

In their ecological model, cells growing away from blood vessels experience intermittent hypoxia and select for upregulated constitutive glycolysis. Exporting lactic acid and protons ($H^+$) lowers the extracellular pH of surrounding tissue: normal somatic cells undergo acid-induced death and matrix metalloproteinases degrade the extracellular matrix, while acid-adapted tumor clones survive and invade the cleared niche (**acid-mediated invasion**).[^1] Glucose competition and pH niche construction thus operate as ecological weapons against adjacent host tissue.

## Evolutionary game theory and adaptive therapy

Working alongside evolutionary ecologist Joel Brown and mathematician Kateřina Staňková, Gatenby paired Lotka–Volterra resource-competition models with evolutionary game theory to show that maximum-tolerated-dose (MTD) chemotherapy triggers an avoidable evolutionary failure: eliminating drug-sensitive cells removes resource competition and induces rapid expansion of resistant lineages (*competitive release*).[^2][^3] Under their adaptive dosing protocol and subsequent Stackelberg game formulations, the clinician acts as a strategic leader modulating treatment intensity so that fitter sensitive clones persist and competitively suppress resistant cells.[^2][^3]

In a 2017 pilot clinical trial of intermittent abiraterone dosing in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, this approach extended median time to progression to at least 27 months while reducing average cumulative drug exposure to roughly 47% of standard continuous therapy.[^4] At Moffitt, Gatenby founded the Integrated Mathematical Oncology program and co-directs the Center of Excellence for Evolutionary Therapy.
