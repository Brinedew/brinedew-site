# ZEB1

ZEB1 is a transcription factor that executes the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT): the developmental program that converts a polarized, immotile epithelial cell into a motile mesenchymal one, and that tumors co-opt to invade and metastasize.

## Function

ZEB1 represses the epithelial program by binding E-box sequences in the promoters of the cadherin genes — E-cadherin (CDH1) above all — and recruiting corepressors that silence them. Loss of E-cadherin dissolves adherens junctions, the cell detaches from its neighbors, and the mesenchymal program (vimentin, N-cadherin, matrix proteases) takes over. ZEB1 sits in double-negative feedback loops with the miR-200 microRNA family — ZEB1 represses miR-200, miR-200 represses ZEB1 — which makes the EMT switch bistable: a cell is in one state or the other, and the transition is a commitment. The same factor maintains stemness in mammary and neural progenitors.

## In cancer

ZEB1 is the transcriptional engine of tumor invasion and metastasis: tumors at the invasive front show nuclear ZEB1, and its expression correlates with metastasis and poor prognosis in colorectal, breast, and pancreatic cancer. ZEB1 also suppresses the epithelial response to [TGF-β](tgfb1-tgfb1-p01137.md)-mediated growth arrest, so EMT converts a cytostatic signal into a motility signal. The miR-200/ZEB1 loop is a candidate target for preventing metastatic seeding.
