# Peter Nowell

![Peter Nowell in his laboratory at the University of Pennsylvania](../Attachments/peter-nowell.jpg)

Peter C. Nowell (1928–2016) was an American pathologist and cancer biologist at the Perelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania, famous for co-discovering the Philadelphia chromosome and formulating the [clonal evolution](clonal-evolution.md) model of cancer.

## Career and honors

Born in 1928, Nowell spent the core of his scientific career at the University of Pennsylvania, where he served as Chair of the Department of Pathology and Laboratory Medicine from 1967 to 1973 and remained active in hematologic malignancy and cytogenetics research for decades.

For establishing the genetic basis of human neoplasia and clonal somatic evolution, he was elected to the **National Academy of Sciences** and the **National Academy of Medicine**, and shared the **1998 Albert Lasker Clinical Medical Research Award** (alongside Janet Rowley and Alfred Knudson).

## The Philadelphia chromosome

In 1960, Nowell and his graduate fellow David Hungerford identified a consistently foreshortened chromosome in leukemic cells from patients with chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) — named the **Philadelphia chromosome** after the city of its discovery. It was the first reproducible chromosomal abnormality linked to any human cancer. Janet Rowley later proved the anomaly resulted from a reciprocal t(9;22) chromosomal translocation creating the *BCR-ABL* fusion oncogene, work that ultimately enabled targeted tyrosine-kinase therapy with imatinib.

## Clonal evolution of cancer

In 1976, Nowell published *"The clonal evolution of tumor cell populations"* in *Science*, synthesis that transformed oncology from descriptive histology into evolutionary biology. He proposed that most neoplasms arise from a single transformed cell and advance through stepwise genetic instability, generating mutant subclones upon which microenvironmental selection and therapy act sequentially. His framework underpins modern branching-evolution genomics, [cancer ecology](cancer-ecology.md), and evolutionary dosing approaches such as [adaptive therapy](adaptive-therapy.md).
