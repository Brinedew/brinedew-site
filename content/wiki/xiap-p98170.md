# XIAP

XIAP (X-linked inhibitor of apoptosis protein) is the only inhibitor-of-apoptosis protein that binds and directly inhibits active caspases, placing it at the final step of the death program rather than an upstream one.

## Function

XIAP's baculoviral IAP repeat (BIR) domains bind caspase-3, caspase-7 and caspase-9: the BIR2 linker region occupies the active site of the executioner caspases, and BIR3 dimerizes and inactivates caspase-9. Its RING domain acts as an E3 ubiquitin ligase that tags bound caspases and its own regulators for degradation. Because [caspase-3](caspase-3-casp3-p42574.md) cleaves XIAP during apoptosis, the inhibitor and its target form a mutual-destruction loop that helps make the death program irreversible once caspase activity passes a threshold.

## In cancer

Tumors overexpressing XIAP resist chemotherapy-induced apoptosis, and high XIAP levels correlate with poor prognosis and drug resistance in leukemia and several carcinomas. Antisense and small-molecule XIAP antagonists (SMAC mimetics, named for the mitochondrial protein SMAC/DIABLO that naturally neutralizes XIAP) have been developed to release caspases in treated tumors.

## Notes

- Human gene symbol: XIAP (also BIRC4); UniProt P98170; 497 amino acids; three BIR domains and a RING finger.
- The gene is X-linked, which explains its name; loss-of-function mutations cause X-linked lymphoproliferative disease type 2.
