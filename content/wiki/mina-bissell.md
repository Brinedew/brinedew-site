# Mina Bissell

![Mina Bissell](../Attachments/mina-bissell.jpg)

**Mina J. Bissell** (born 1940) is an Iranian-American cell biologist and Distinguished Scientist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, known for demonstrating that three-dimensional tissue architecture and the extracellular matrix (ECM) regulate nuclear gene expression and can dominate the oncogenic phenotype.[^1][^2] At Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, she also recruited [Judith Campisi](judith-campisi.md) in 1990.

## Dynamic reciprocity

Challenging models that treated malignant behavior as determined solely by cell-autonomous DNA mutations, Bissell, H. Glenn Hall, and Gordon Parry proposed the principle of **dynamic reciprocity**.[^1] In this framework, the extracellular matrix acts as an active signaling boundary rather than inert scaffolding: ECM proteins transmit physical and biochemical signals through transmembrane integrins and the cytoskeleton to the nuclear matrix and chromatin to direct polarity, differentiation, and gene transcription, while cells reciprocally secrete and remodel the surrounding matrix.[^1]

## The HMT-3522 model and malignant reversion

Bissell's laboratory tested dynamic reciprocity using three-dimensional reconstituted basement-membrane assays and the HMT-3522 human breast epithelial progression series, spanning non-malignant `S1` cells and tumorigenic `T4-2` cells derived without carcinogens or viral oncogenes:[^2]

- Whereas `S1` and `T4-2` cells look superficially similar on flat plastic dishes, inside three-dimensional laminin-rich matrices `S1` cells assemble polarized, growth-arrested mammary acini, while `T4-2` cells form disorganized proliferative masses.[^2]
- Treating tumorigenic `T4-2` cells in three-dimensional matrix with $\beta_1$-integrin-blocking antibodies—or normalizing hyperactive [EGFR](egfr-erbb1-p00533.md) signaling—restored polarized acinar organization and arrested malignant growth *in vitro* and *in vivo* without correcting the cells' underlying genomic mutations.[^2]

These phenotypic reversion experiments demonstrated that tissue context can override oncogenic genotype, linking extracellular mechanotransduction ([YAP1](yap-yap1.md)/[TAZ](taz-wwtr1.md)) to evolutionary models of cancer as disrupted tissue constraint ([multicellular cooperation](multicellular-cooperation.md)).

## Honors

Bissell has been elected to the National Academy of Sciences and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and received the E. B. Wilson Medal of the American Society for Cell Biology.
