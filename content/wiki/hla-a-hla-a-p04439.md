# HLA-A

HLA-A is a class I major histocompatibility complex molecule: the cell-surface display protein that presents intracellular peptide fragments to CD8 T cells, making the interior of every cell visible to immune surveillance.

## Function

HLA-A binds short peptides (8–10 amino acids) generated by the proteasome from the cell's own proteins — including proteins from viruses and from mutated oncogenes — and carries them to the cell surface for inspection by CD8 T cells. A T cell whose receptor recognizes a presented peptide as foreign kills the displaying cell. Each person expresses three HLA-A alleles (one from each parent, out of thousands in the population), and the allele set determines which peptides an individual can present, which is the molecular basis of tissue-transplant matching and of individual variation in antiviral and antitumor immunity.

## In cancer

Tumor cells reduce HLA-A display — through loss of heterozygosity, down-regulation of the processing machinery, or viral interference — because a cell presenting tumor-antigen peptides is a target for cytotoxic T cells. Reduced HLA class I expression is one of the standard escape phenotypes of [immunoediting](immune-surveillance.md), and it explains why tumors with low HLA respond poorly to [checkpoint inhibitors](pd-1-pdcd1.md): the brake can be released only if the tumor still displays its antigens.
