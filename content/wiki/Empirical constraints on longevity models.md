# Empirical constraints on longevity models

| Name                                 | Observation                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Which models are ruled out                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frailty in nature                    | Animals that are elderly and frail are observed in nature, not just in the lab                                                                                                                                 | Naive [[selection-shadow-theory\|selection shadow theory]]: *natural selection is blind to late-life frailty alleles and doesn't get a chance to purge them*                                                                          |
| Gompertz law                         | After sexual maturity, adult mortality rate rises roughly exponentially with chronological age in humans and most vertebrates                                                                                    | [[Mortality rate models\|Constant-hazard / pure extrinsic-mortality models]] that treat adult death risk as age-invariant                                                                                                              |
| [[ctvt-line\|CTVT]]                  | A dog transmissible cancer cell line ([[ctvt-line\|CTVT]]) has persisted for 10,000+ years                                                                                                                     | Naive intracellular accumulation/depletion models: *all cells become worse over organismal lifespan because evolution couldn't find a way to preserve a good substance or cleanse a bad substance*                                   |
| Immortal germline                    | Zygote-germline-zygote cycle has persisted unbroken for 1 billion+ years                                                                                                                                       | Naive intracellular accumulation/depletion models                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| [[petos-paradox\|Peto's paradox]]    | Across mammals spanning >10,000-fold body mass and >30-fold lifespan, lifetime cancer mortality does not scale linearly with cell count × lifespan                                                             | Naive cell-division-counting mutation models without lineage-specific evolution of enhanced tumor suppression                                                                                                                          |
| Serial cloning                       | In a mouse line maintained exclusively by serial SCNT cloning (Wakayama et al.), cloning efficiency remained stable for ~25 generations, declined from generation 27, and became inviable by generation 58     | Models assuming asexual somatic nuclear reprogramming can bypass Muller's ratchet indefinitely without meiotic/sexual purging                                                                                                           |
| Common traits of long-living species | Species known for long lifespans tend to be: 1. Low predation risk 2. Eusocial 3. Intelligent 4. Large 5. K-strategists                                                                                         | Single-factor wear-and-tear or raw mass-only scaling models: low extrinsic hazard shifts selection toward somatic maintenance and [[tumor-suppressor-theory-of-aging\|anti-cancer restraint]]                                           |
| Social insect longevity dimorphism   | In social insects, queens and workers share the same genome, yet queens (who reproduce) live 10–30× longer than workers (who don't reproduce)                                                                  | Naive [[disposable-soma-theory\|disposable soma theory]]: *in all species, reproduction is in a rigid physiological resource trade-off with longevity*                                                                                 |
| Avian and bat longevity              | Birds and bats feature active metabolism, higher body temperatures than rodents of comparable mass, and yet live many times longer than rodents                                                                | Naive "rate of living" theories: *species that run their internals faster also wear out faster*                                                                                                                                        |
| End-of-life somatic mutation burden  | In intestinal crypts among 16 mammalian species (Cagan et al., 2022), end-of-life mutation count per cell converged near ~3,000 despite huge variations in body mass and lifespan                               | Fixed somatic clock / passive polymerase-error models assuming all mammals accumulate mutations at the same annual rate                                                                                                              |
| Octopus lifespan                     | Despite large brains and complex behavior, semelparous octopuses die within 1–5 years shortly after reproducing; removing the optic gland halts post-maternal starvation and extends life (Wodinsky, 1977)     | Purely passive stochastic-damage theories: cephalopod post-reproductive collapse is an acute, active endocrine program (optic gland steroid/cholesterol signaling; Wang et al., 2022)                                                   |
| Antioxidant unresponsiveness         | Overexpression of antioxidant enzymes fails to extend mammalian lifespan                                                                                                                                       | Free radical theory of aging                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Caloric restriction longevity        | Many lifespan records across model species are achieved under caloric restriction                                                                                                                              | Naive [[disposable-soma-theory\|disposable soma theory]] predicting starvation must uniformly shorten somatic maintenance                                                                                                              |
| Dwarf longevity                      | Within the same species (e.g., Ames *Prop1<sup>df/df</sup>* and Snell *Pit1<sup>dw/dw</sup>* mice living 40–50% longer, and small-breed dogs), dwarf strains with reduced GH/[[igf-1\|IGF-1]] signaling live longer | Models treating higher somatic growth rate or stronger anabolic GH/[[igf-1\|IGF-1]] signaling as uniformly pro-longevity                                                                                                              |
| Heterochronic parabiosis             | Prolonged heterochronic parabiosis followed by detachment (Zhang et al., 2023) reverses multi-omic age signatures and extends median and maximum mouse lifespan                                                | Strictly cell-autonomous, irreversible intracellular clock models assuming circulating systemic factors cannot alter aged cellular state or lifespan                                                                                     |

## Primary evidence referenced
- Cagan, A. et al. (2022). Somatic mutation rates scale with lifespan across mammals. *Nature*.
- Wang, Z. Y. et al. (2022). Steroid hormones of the octopus self-destruct system. *Current Biology*. See also Wodinsky, J. (1977). Hormonal inhibition of feeding and death in *Octopus*: control by optic gland secretion. *Science*, 198(4320), 948–951.
- Zhang, B., Lee, D. E., Trapp, A., White, J. M., Gladyshev, V. N. et al. (2023). Multi-omic rejuvenation and life span extension on exposure to youthful circulation. *Nature Aging*, 3, 948–964.
