# Antler regeneration

Deer antlers are the only mammalian organs that completely regenerate every year, growing up to 2.75 centimeters per day.
***
Key Stages of Regeneration

- **Casting (Winter/Early Spring):** Declining testosterone levels trigger osteoclasts (bone-resorbing cells) to break down the connection at the pedicle—the permanent bony base on the deer's skull—causing the old hard antlers to drop off.

- **Blastema Formation & Rapid Growth (Spring/Summer):** Specialized stem cells called **Antler Blastema Progenitor Cells (ABPCs)** activate in the pedicle periosteum. They rapidly multiply and rebuild bone, cartilage, blood vessels, nerves, and skin.

- **Velvet Stage:** The growing structure is covered in a soft, vascular skin known as "velvet," which supplies nutrient-rich blood to the fast-developing bone.

- **Calcification and Shedding of Velvet (Late Summer/Autumn):** Rising testosterone levels calcify the bone, cut off blood flow to the velvet, and cause the deer to rub the dry velvet off against trees in preparation for the mating season (rut)

Unique Biological Features

- **Extreme Growth Speed:** Antlers can reach up to 15 kg of bone and tissue in just 3 to 4 months, making them the fastest-growing animal tissue known.

- **Trophic Memory:** Research has found that if an antler is injured or scarred, the deer's body can "remember" the trauma site and reproduce a matching branch or feature in the exact same location the following year.

- **Regenerative Healing & Stem Cells:** Unlike normal mammalian skin wounds that leave scars, forehead skin during the antler growth period heals completely scar-free. Scientists study ABPCs and extracellular vesicles for potential applications in human tissue repair and anti-aging research
