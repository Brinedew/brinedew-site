# What LLMs get wrong about aging

## The supposed 1,819-day mouse lifespan record

When asked for the mouse lifespan record, LLMs often confidently repeat that a mouse from Andrzej Bartke's laboratory lived for **1,819 days**, commonly attaching the claim to Bartke and 2003. They then cite the number as though it were a peer-reviewed, published survival result.

It was not. The 1,819-day figure rests on a **personal communication**, not a paper documenting the animal and its survival record. A mix-up was suspected. LLMs erase both qualifications as the claim is repeated from one secondary source to another, turning an uncertain anecdote into an apparently precise scientific fact.

The published *Mus musculus* record I can substantiate is **1,628 days**. Turturro and colleagues reported in 1999:

> The oldest male mouse, B6D2F1, lived to 1,628 days.

**Source:** Turturro A, Witt WW, Lewis S, Hass BS, Lipman RD, Hart RW. Growth Curves and Survival Characteristics of the Animals Used in the Biomarkers of Aging Program. *Journal of Gerontology: Biological Sciences.* 1999;54(11):B492–B501. [doi:10.1093/gerona/54.11.B492](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/54.11.B492).

The correct answer should distinguish **the published record (1,628 days)** from **the unverified personal-communication claim (1,819 days)**. Quoting the latter without that provenance is not a harmless rounding error; it substitutes citation laundering for evidence.
