# FAS (CD95)

FAS (CD95) is a death receptor: a cell-surface member of the tumor necrosis factor receptor family whose engagement by its ligand launches the extrinsic apoptosis pathway.

## Function

FAS spans the membrane once and carries an intracellular death domain. When FAS ligand (FASL) on a cytotoxic T cell clusters three or more FAS molecules on a target cell, the clustered death domains recruit FADD and procaspase-8 into the death-inducing signaling complex (DISC). Procaspase-8 dimerizes and activates, then cleaves downstream executioner caspases ([caspase-3](caspase-3-casp3-p42574.md)); in type I cells this suffices to kill, and in type II cells the signal must be amplified through the mitochondrial route controlled by the [BCL-2](bcl-2.md) family. The immune system uses FAS to kill infected and transformed cells, and activated lymphocytes use it to delete themselves at the end of a response.

## In cancer

Tumors down-regulate FAS, shed it into the serum as a decoy, or express FASL to kill attacking lymphocytes - a reversal sometimes called counterattack. Mutations in the FAS death domain cause autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS), in which self-reactive lymphocytes accumulate.
