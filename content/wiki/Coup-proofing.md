# Coup-proofing

**Coup-proofing** refers to a set of political and military strategies used by authoritarian or insecure regimes to minimize the risk of a coup d'état—an elite-led or military-led overthrow of a leader. 
***
Common Coup-Proofing Strategies

- **Parallel Militaries:** Creating a separate elite military force (such as a presidential guard, republican guard, or specialized militia) that is better funded, loyal directly to the leader, and acts as a counterweight to the regular army.

- **Overlapping Security Agencies:** Establishing multiple, competing intelligence and internal security services that monitor both the regular military and each other to prevent coordinated plots. 

- **Family, Ethnic, or Religious Nepotism:** Placing trusted family members, or specific ethnic/religious minority groups loyal to the regime, in key military and command positions.

- **Officer Purges and Rotation:** Frequently rotating commanders, purging suspicious officers, or keeping military units small and restricting their access to ammunition to prevent power bases from forming.

- **Civilianization of Posts:** Appointing loyal civilians to defense and interior cabinet positions rather than independent military figures. 

Consequences of Coup-Proofing

- **Reduced Military Effectiveness:** Prioritizing political loyalty and affinity over merit, skill, or experience degrades the overall combat capability and flexibility of the regular armed forces. 

- **Deepened Social Divides:** Reinforcing ethnic, sectarian, or political fault lines within the state can fracture national unity.

- **High Financial Cost:** Diverting significant state budgets toward patronage, parallel forces, and inflated security apparatuses drains economic resources.
