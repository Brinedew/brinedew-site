# Multicellular cooperation

**Multicellular cooperation** is the set of coordinated cellular behaviors that maintain a multicellular organism as a single evolutionary individual: proliferative restraint, regulated cell death, controlled resource uptake, division of labor, and maintenance of the shared extracellular environment.[^1]

## Cooperation as enforceable agreements

Evolutionary biologists [Athena Aktipis](athena-aktipis.md) and Randolph Nesse framed these five behaviors as enforceable evolutionary agreements among cells rather than automatic harmony, because somatic cells still retain metabolic and replicative pathways inherited from free-living unicellular ancestors.[^1] Enforcement operates across overlapping host defenses—cell-intrinsic arrest and repair ([p53](p53-tp53.md)), programmed [apoptosis](apoptosis.md) ([APAF1](apaf1.md) and caspases), replicative limits ([telomeres](telomeres.md) and [replicative credit](replicative-credit.md)), and extrinsic [immune surveillance](immune-surveillance.md)—each raising the evolutionary cost of somatic defection.

Cellular cooperation is also shaped by **life-history trade-offs**: traits that favor rapid proliferation, tissue invasion, and motility during wound healing or reproduction mirror phenotypes expressed in malignancy when freed from tissue control, while longer-lived species invest more heavily in policing somatic defectors.[^2]

## Cancer as cheating and spatial modeling

Within this framework, cancer arises when somatic lineages defect from multicellular agreements—proliferating without mitogenic signals, evading death cues, monopolizing diffusing nutrients, abandoning differentiated tissue functions, and remodeling or acidifying the extracellular matrix for clonal gain.[^1][^3] Selection within tissues amplifies successful defectors, producing the conflict captured by [multilevel selection](multilevel-selection.md): a cell lineage wins short-term selection inside the body while degrading the host organism.[^3]

Agent-based spatial simulations modeling cooperating and defecting cells in resource-diffusing tissues establish two core principles:[^3]

1. **Spatial viscosity and local resource sharing** strongly constrain cheating: when public goods remain localized and defecting cells exhaust their immediate patch, cheater spread slows relative to well-mixed populations.
2. **Cooperation-informed therapy** targets the social ecology of the tumor—modifying resource supply, penalizing cheater phenotypes, or preserving drug-sensitive cooperating clones ([adaptive therapy](adaptive-therapy.md), [cancer ecology](cancer-ecology.md)) rather than attempting indiscriminate eradication.

Viewed through [evolutionary transitions in individuality](evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality.md), malignant transformation represents a partial reversion from integrated multicellular agency back toward single-cell competition.[^1][^3]
