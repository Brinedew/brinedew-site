# Multicellular cooperation

Multicellular cooperation is the set of coordinated behaviors that keep a cellular collective viable as a single organism: restraint of proliferation, regulated cell death, controlled resource uptake, division of labor, and maintenance of the shared extracellular environment.

## Cooperation as enforceable agreements

[Athena Aktipis](athena-aktipis.md) and Randolph Nesse (*Evolutionary Applications*, 2013) framed these five behaviors as evolutionary agreements among cells — agreements that only hold when enforced by host mechanisms, because every cell still carries metabolic and replicative machinery inherited from independent unicellular ancestors. Enforcement operates across multiple layers: cell-intrinsic arrest and repair ([p53](p53-tp53.md)), programmed [apoptosis](apoptosis.md) ([APAF1](apaf1.md), caspases), proliferative counting ([telomeres](telomeres.md), [replicative credit](replicative-credit.md)), and extrinsic [immune surveillance](immune-surveillance.md). Each constraint raises the fitness cost of somatic defection.

With Amy Boddy, [Robert Gatenby](robert-gatenby.md), Joel Brown, and [Carlo Maley](carlo-maley.md), Aktipis (*Nature Reviews Cancer*, 2013) further tied cellular cooperation to evolutionary **life-history trade-offs**: traits that favor rapid somatic proliferation, invasion, and motility during injury repair or reproductive maturity mirror the very behaviors that become malignant when uncoupled from tissue control, while longer-lived hosts invest disproportionately in restraining somatic defectors.

## Cancer as cheating and spatial modeling

In this framing, cancer arises when somatic lineages cheat on those multicellular agreements — dividing without signals, ignoring death cues, monopolizing diffusing nutrients, abandoning differentiated tissue work, and acidifying or remodeling the matrix to their own advantage. Selection within the tissue amplifies successful defectors, producing the core conflict of [multilevel selection](multilevel-selection.md): a cell lineage winning short-term selection inside the body while degrading the organismal level above it.

To test when cooperators contain or succumb to defectors, Aktipis and collaborators built spatial agent-based simulations (including NetLogo and lattice cellular models) representing cooperating and defecting cell agents embedded in resource-diffusing tissues. These simulations demonstrate two robust principles:

1. **Spatial viscosity and local resource sharing** strongly constrain cheating: when public goods (growth factors, matrix protection) remain local and defective neighbors exhaust their immediate patch, cheater spread slows compared to well-mixed systems.
2. **Cooperation-informed therapy** targets the shared social dynamics — altering resource supply, penalizing cheater phenotypes, or preserving cooperating and less aggressive clones ([adaptive therapy](adaptive-therapy.md), [cancer ecology](cancer-ecology.md)) rather than indiscriminately eradicating cells.

This connects oncology directly to [evolutionary transitions in individuality](evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality.md): somatic neoplasms are partial reversions from integrated multicellular agency back toward single-cell competition.
