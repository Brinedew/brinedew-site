# Evolutionary transitions in individuality

Evolutionary transitions in individuality are events in which groups of previously independent replicators become new evolutionary individuals: genes into chromosomes, prokaryotic cells into eukaryotic cells, single cells into multicellular organisms, solitary organisms into societies.

## The framework

[John Maynard Smith](john-maynard-smith.md) and [Eörs Szathmáry](eors-szathmary.md) organized the history of life around this sequence in *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (1995). Each transition changes how hereditary information is stored and transmitted, and each raises the same two questions: why do the parts cooperate, and what stops a part from defecting?

## What a transition requires

[Leo Buss](leo-buss.md) argued in *The Evolution of Individuality* (1987) that the obstacle is conflict among cell lineages, and that developmental mechanisms — a single-cell bottleneck, germline sequestration — evolved to suppress it. [Richard Michod](richard-michod.md) formulated the same process in fitness terms: during a transition, fitness exports from the lower-level units to the collective, and the collective mediates conflict among its parts. Godfrey-Smith's [de-Darwinization](de-darwinization.md) framework describes the same requirements in philosophical vocabulary: the parts must lose variation, heritability, and differential fitness of their own.

The transitions framework is the standard context for treating cancer as a failure of individuality — a reversion of cells to independent Darwinian behavior within a body that evolved to suppress it.
