# Anti-frontrunner social dynamics

**"Anti-frontrunner" dynamics** refer to how groups react when one individual visibly pulls ahead in competence, ambition, or status.

Autistic thinkers frequently struggle to intuitively understand and navigate these dynamics because their default social model is often **meritocratic, object-level, and 1v1** (performance = reward), whereas large human groups operate on **n-player cooperative-adversarial game theory**.

---

### 1. Multiplayer vs. 1v1

In a 1v1 match (like chess or a math test), the highest-performing player wins. However, in multiplayer games with communication and shifting alliances—such as *Diplomacy*, *Risk*, or corporate hierarchy—**visibly pulling ahead is a losing strategy**. 

* **The Math of the Coalition:** If 10 peers are competing for a single promotion and one is an undeniable 10x star performer, the other 9 have two choices:
  1. *Work 10x harder to outcompete them* (statistically unlikely).
  2. *Form an informal anti-frontrunner coalition to eliminate the star* (low effort, high probability of success).
* **The Autistic Blindspot:** Autistic individuals often assume that delivering undeniable, objective value makes them indispensable and secure. They miss that in a multi-agent system, high competence without political cover makes them the single biggest **Schelling point** for everyone else to target.

---

### 2. Anti-Frontrunner Strategies

When a group mobilizes against an outperforming peer, they rarely attack their actual work; they attack the social flank using several distinct strategies:

#### A. Weaponized "Culture Fit" and "Not a Team Player"
Because the frontrunner’s raw output cannot be challenged on the merits, the coalition shifts the criteria of evaluation entirely from **competence** to **coordination and vibes**.
* **The Tactic:** Peer reviews and hallway gossip frame the frontrunner’s high velocity as "erratic," "uncollaborative," or "difficult to work with."
* **Why it fails to grok:** To someone who communicates literally, "team player" means doing one's job exceptionally well so the team wins. They struggle to realize that in political terms, "team player" often means *not making your peers look incompetent or redundant*.

#### B. Leveling Mechanisms
As anthropologist Christopher Boehm documented in hunter-gatherer bands, groups naturally develop collective mechanisms to suppress anyone who attempts to dominate or stand out (ridicule, ostracism, sabotage). In modern terms, this is **Tall Poppy Syndrome**.
* **The Tactic:** Small social slights, gossip, and collective cooling off designed to bring the "tall poppy" back to the average baseline.
* **Why it fails to grok:** Autistic people often see efficiency and optimization as moral goods. They do not realize that human social units frequently prioritize **group cohesion and threat minimization** over aggregate performance.

#### C. Bureaucratic Process Traps
* **The Tactic:** Middle managers and rival peers will deliberately tie down a high-output individual with procedural friction—more meetings, sign-off chains, and documentation hoops. 
* **Why it fails to grok:** The autistic contributor often responds by trying to follow the rules even more literally or arguing against the procedural stupidity, failing to see that the rules were never about quality assurance; they were designed specifically to choke their speed.

---

### 3. Survival Strategies Frontrunners Use

Neurotypical individuals who successfully maintain a frontrunner position without being destroyed use specific defensive strategies that many autistic individuals find deeply unnatural, deceptive, or counterintuitive:

* **Strategic Sandbagging (Playing Below Your Power):** Hiding your actual capabilities or progress until the very last moment to avoid drawing early aggro. Autistic people usually prefer to work at maximum capacity and deliver results immediately; holding back feels dishonest or inefficient.
* **Strategic Self-Deprecation & Feigning Harmlessness:** Deliberately showing small, endearing flaws, asking peers for advice on trivial matters, or downplaying achievements so that peers do not feel their own status is under threat. Autistic communication tends to favor unvarnished, accurate facts; minimizing their own achievements feels like lying, and asking for help when they don't need it feels absurd.
* **Credit Laundering (Taxing Your Wins for Allies):** Generously handing out co-credit to peers who contributed little or nothing. This aligns incentives: by giving potential rivals a cut of the glory, they are incentivized to protect the frontrunner rather than coordinate against them. An autistic mindset often views credit distribution as an exact ledger of labor and truth, rejecting credit-sharing as unearned patronage.
* **Under-the-Radar Alliances:** Ensuring that before making any visible move or pitching an ambitious project, you have individually pre-cleared it with key stakeholders in 1-on-1 private channels. Autistic workers frequently pitch things cold in public forums, assuming a good idea will win on logic, inadvertently exposing themselves to immediate group counter-coalition.
