# Michael Levin

![Michael Levin](../Attachments/michael-levin.jpg)

**Michael Levin** is an American developmental and synthetic biologist, Vannevar Bush Professor of Biology at Tufts University, where he directs the Allen Discovery Center and the Tufts Center for Regenerative and Developmental Biology. His research examines [bioelectric signaling](bioelectricity.md), basal cognition, and the biophysical encoding of large-scale anatomical form during morphogenesis.

## Bioelectric pattern memory and regeneration

Levin's laboratory studies how spatiotemporal gradients of transmembrane resting voltage ($V_{\text{mem}}$) and gap-junctional coupling across non-neural somatic tissues encode anatomical target morphologies. In *Xenopus laevis* embryos, imposing eye-field-like $V_{\text{mem}}$ patterns on distant somatic regions induces formed ectopic eyes outside the head.[^1] In regenerating planarian flatworms, transient gap-junction blockade resets head–tail polarity into permanent two-headed animals that continue regenerating two heads in plain water without genetic alteration. Behavioral conditioning experiments further showed that conditioned planarians retain learned photophobic navigation memories even after decapitation and regeneration of a new brain from the tail fragment, pointing to extra-neural physiological information storage across regenerating tissue.[^2]

## "Cognitive glue," morphospace, and cancer

Levin models organisms as nested, multiscale collectives navigating anatomical state space (**morphospace**):

- **Cognitive glue.** Gap junctions, bioelectric networks, and biochemical error signals integrate locally autonomous cells into a larger multicellular collective capable of pursuing tissue- and organ-level anatomical targets.
- **Cognitive light cone.** In Levin's terminology, the spatiotemporal boundary over which a living system senses deviations and executes homeostatic correction scales up during [evolutionary transitions in individuality](evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality.md) as physiological coupling expands from cellular microns to whole-organism anatomy.
- **Cancer as goal-scale contraction.** From this perspective, tumorigenesis reflects a loss of intercellular gap-junctional integration: an electrically uncoupled cell contracts its homeostatic boundary back to single-cell proliferation and survival, treating host tissue as an external environment. Enforcing membrane hyperpolarization across oncogene-expressing frog embryonic tissues suppresses tumor formation and restores cooperative organization without editing DNA driver mutations.[^3]

The laboratory's synthetic biology studies, including motile multicellular frog constructs (*Xenobots*), explore how genetic wild-type cells assemble novel functional morphologies outside standard embryonic paths.
