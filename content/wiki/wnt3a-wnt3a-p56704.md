# WNT3A

WNT3A is a secreted Wnt ligand: a signaling protein that binds Frizzled receptors on neighboring cells and activates the canonical Wnt pathway, whose central effector is [β-catenin](beta-catenin-ctnnb1.md).

## Function

WNT3A is lipid-modified (palmitoleoylated by PORCN), which both confines it to short-range signaling and is required for its secretion by Wntless. Bound to Frizzled and LRP5/6 at the receiving cell's membrane, it disables the β-catenin destruction complex, and Wnt target genes turn on. WNT3A maintains stem-cell compartments - intestinal crypts, hematopoietic stem cells - and patterns tissues during development, where Wnt gradients are among the morphogen systems that organize the body plan.

## In cancer

Overproduction of Wnt ligands is one route to pathway activation in tumors that keep their APC and CTNNB1 genes intact - pancreatic, breast, and lung cancers secrete Wnt ligands to sustain their own stem-like compartments and their stroma. WNT3A in the tumor microenvironment also acts on macrophages and fibroblasts, recruiting them into a tumor-supporting state. Pharmacological Wnt inhibition has been limited by the pathway's role in the gut, where blocking it destroys the intestinal epithelium.

## Notes

- Human gene symbol: WNT3A; UniProt P56704; 352 amino acids (precursor); Wnt family, 19 members in mammals.
- The name joins "Wingless" (the *Drosophila* gene) and "Int-1" (the mouse integration-site oncogene) - the same gene found twice.
