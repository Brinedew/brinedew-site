# Hamilton's rule


Hamilton's rule (rB > C) explains when natural selection favors altruistic or cooperative behavior toward genetic relatives (kin selection).
***
The Formula

\(rB>C\)

- **r (Coefficient of Relatedness):** The fraction of genes shared, on average, between the altruist (actor) and the recipient. (e.g., r = 0.5 for siblings or parent-offspring, r = 0.25 for grandparents/grandchildren or uncles/aunts).

- **B (Benefit to Recipient):** The reproductive benefit (measured in the extra number of offspring equivalents) gained by the individual being helped.

- **C (Cost to Altruist):** The reproductive cost (measured in the number of fewer offspring produced) suffered by the individual performing the altruistic act. 

---

Core Concept

- **Altruism Evolves When:** The weighted benefit to the relative exceeds the direct cost to yourself (rB > C). Even if an individual sacrifices their own direct reproduction (C), the shared genes inside the relative are passed on in large enough numbers to increase **inclusive fitness**. 

- **Real-World Examples:**
    - **Alarm Calls:** Ground squirrels or meerkats risk drawing a predator's attention to themselves to warn close relatives in the group.
    - **Eusocial Insects:** Worker honeybees or ants forgo their own reproduction completely to raise sisters, where high relatedness (r = 0.75 due to haplodiploidy) makes helping the queen produce more sisters evolutionarily advantageous
