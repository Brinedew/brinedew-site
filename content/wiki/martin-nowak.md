# Martin Nowak

![Martin Nowak](../Attachments/martin-nowak.jpg)

**Martin A. Nowak** (born 1965) is an Austrian mathematical biologist, professor of biology and of mathematics at Harvard University, where he directs the Program for Evolutionary Dynamics. His research builds mathematical models of the [evolution of cooperation](evolution-of-cooperation.md), within-host viral evolution, and somatic carcinogenesis.

## Viral quasispecies and HIV antigenic diversity

Trained in Manfred Eigen's quasispecies tradition in Vienna, Nowak early applied evolutionary population dynamics to viral infection inside individual hosts. Modeling HIV as an evolving swarm of antigenic escape mutants with Roy Anderson, Robert May, and collaborators, he showed that immune responses suppress low viral diversity during the asymptomatic phase until accumulating escape variants cross an **antigenic diversity threshold**, exceeding the finite capacity of T-cell control and driving progression to AIDS.[^1]

## Evolution of cooperation and evolutionary dynamics

With Karl Sigmund, Nowak developed mathematical models of indirect reciprocity via reputation (**image scoring**), demonstrating how third-party observation sustains cooperation when interaction histories spread across groups.[^2][^3] Later graph-theoretic work showed that on structured networks with degree $k$, natural selection favors cooperation whenever $b/c > k$,[^4] and Nowak organized these mechanisms with kin selection, direct reciprocity, and group selection into five formal rules for cooperation.[^5]

His textbook *Evolutionary Dynamics: Exploring the Equations of Life* (2006) integrates quasispecies dynamics, finite-population Moran processes, tissue fixation probabilities, and evolutionary game theory;[^6] with Roger Highfield, he presented this program for general readers in *SuperCooperators* (2011).[^7]

## Somatic evolution of cancer

Working with Franziska Michor, Yoh Iwasa, and others, Nowak modeled tumor initiation in structured cell compartments, chronic myeloid leukemia progression and imatinib resistance, chromosomal instability, and two-hit tumor-suppressor inactivation in crypt stem-cell architectures. This formalism links spatial tissue structure directly to [multicellular cooperation](multicellular-cooperation.md) and [multilevel selection](multilevel-selection.md).

Nowak previously served as Professor of Mathematical Biology at the University of Oxford and led theoretical biology research at the Institute for Advanced Study in Vienna before joining Harvard University in 2003.
