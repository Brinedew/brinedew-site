# MCL1 (MCL-1)

MCL-1 is an anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family member distinguished by its short protein half-life, which makes it the family's rapid-response survival factor.

## Function

MCL-1 binds and neutralizes the pore-formers [BAX](bax.md) and [BAK](bak-bak1.md), with a binding pattern that overlaps [BCL-2](bcl-2.md) and [BCL-xL](bcl-xl-bcl2l1.md) but matches neither exactly — many cell types depend on MCL-1 specifically, and the BH3-only proteins MCL-1 sequesters differ from those held by its relatives. Because the protein turns over within hours, MCL-1 levels track the cell's current growth-factor supply: withdrawal of survival signals drops MCL-1 quickly, and the cell becomes primed for death. This lability is why MCL-1 is the anti-apoptotic protein most often recruited by tumors under treatment pressure.

## In cancer

Amplification of the MCL1 gene is among the most frequent copy-number gains in human cancer, and tumors that acquire resistance to [BCL-2](bcl-2.md) inhibitors commonly switch to MCL-1 dependence. The dependence runs both ways: cancer cell lines die rapidly when MCL-1 is degraded, which has made MCL-1 inhibitors and MCL-1 degraders an active drug-discovery area.
