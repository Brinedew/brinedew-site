# Glossary

## A

- **Agent-based model (ABM)**: A simulation approach where individual agents (such as cells) follow rules whose interactions produce emergent tissue-level behavior.
- **Angiogenesis**: Formation of new blood vessels that supply nutrients and oxygen to growing tissues and tumors.
- **Anoikis**: Programmed cell suicide triggered when cells lose appropriate attachment to the extracellular matrix.
- **[[antagonistic-pleiotropy-theory|Antagonistic pleiotropy]]**: The idea that traits beneficial early in life can have harmful late-life effects such as aging or cancer risk.
- **Apoptosis (intrinsic)**: A cell suicide pathway activated by the cell itself after intracellular stress or damage.
- **Apoptosis (extrinsic)**: A cell suicide pathway activated by other cells
- **Autophagy**: An intracellular recycling process that degrades damaged components to maintain homeostasis or survive stress.

## B

- **[[bcl-2|BCL-2]]**: An anti-apoptotic mitochondrial membrane protein that blocks cell death and can enable therapy resistance.
- **[[beta-catenin-ctnnb1|Beta-catenin]]**: A Wnt pathway mediator that regulates gene expression and can drive proliferation when stabilized.

## C

- **[[ctvt|Canine transmissible venereal tumor (CTVT)]]**: A naturally transmissible cancer lineage that spreads between dogs by cell transfer.
- **[[caspase-3-casp3-p42574|Caspase-3]]**: An executioner protease in apoptosis that cleaves many substrates to dismantle the cell.
- **Cell competition**: A process where fitter cells eliminate less-fit neighbors, helping maintain tissue quality.
- **[[cellular-senescence|Cellular senescence]]**: Irreversible cell-cycle arrest with characteristic secretory changes that suppress tumors but can impair tissue function with age.
- **Chemokines**: Small secreted proteins that guide immune cell migration and shape inflammatory responses.
- **Chemotaxis**: Directed cell movement along chemical gradients of attractants or repellents.
- **Clonal cooperation**: Interactions between distinct cell clones that collectively enhance growth, survival, or invasion beyond what single clones achieve.
- **Contact inhibition**: Suppression of cell proliferation or migration upon cell crowding or direct cell contact in healthy tissues.
- **Crowding pressure**: Mechanical or spatial constraints that cells experience in a densely packed tissue.
- **Cytokines**: Signaling proteins, often immune-derived, that modulate inflammation, growth, and cell survival.

## D

- **[[death-pact|Death pact]]**: A governance rule that enforces organization-level priorities by triggering collective agent death under specific failures or contexts.
- **[[de-darwinization|De-Darwinization]]**: An evolutionary pressure to reduce competition among cells in a multicellular organism.
- **[[dftd|Devil facial tumor disease (DFTD)]]**: A transmissible cancer threatening Tasmanian devils that spreads by biting.
- **DNA damage response (DDR)**: A cellular network that senses DNA lesions and coordinates repair, checkpoint activation, or cell death.
- **[[disposable-soma-theory|Disposable soma theory]]**: The idea that limited resources favor reproduction over long-term somatic maintenance, contributing to aging.
- **Driver mutation**: A genetic change to a cell within a tissue that confers it a fitness advantage over its peers and promotes cancer development.

## E

- **[[e2f1-e2f1-q01094|E2F transcription factors]]**: Proteins that drive expression of genes required for mitosis S-phase entry and are restrained by the RB pathway.
- **ECM remodeling**: Changes to the extracellular matrix that alter tissue stiffness, signaling, and cell behavior, often via matrix metalloproteinases.
- **EMT (epithelial-mesenchymal transition)**: A cell-state change that increases motility and invasiveness at the expense of epithelial traits.
- **[[eutely|Eutely]]**: A developmental pattern in some species where adults have a fixed number of somatic cells and growth occurs only by cell enlargement.
- **[[egfr-erbb1-p00533|EGFR signaling]]**: A receptor tyrosine kinase pathway activated by EGF that drives proliferation and survival and is often dysregulated in cancer.
- **Extracellular matrix**: a part of tissue outside of the cells, consisting of a network of macromolecules and minerals that provides structural and biochemical support to surrounding cells.

## F

- **Fast-slow scheduling**: A performance optimization that updates some processes less frequently to speed simulation without losing key dynamics.
- **[[flower-drosophila|Flower (Drosophila)]]**: A cell-surface protein that marks relative fitness for cell competition in flies.

## G

- **Gene regulatory network (GRN)**: A computational model of a gene network that computes internal gene states that drive effector outputs based on sensory inputs in the cell model.
- **Genome instability**: The measure of how quickly the genome of a cell in a multicellular body accumulates mutations and chromosomal alterations that drive somatic evolution.
- **Gradient (chemical)**: A spatial variation in morphogen concentration that cells can sense to bias movement or growth.
- **Growth factors**: Extracellular proteins such as EGF and IGF that bind to cell receptors and stimulate cell proliferation and survival pathways.

## H

- **[[hela|HeLa cells]]**: An immortalized human cancer cell line widely used in research.
- **[[shh-shh-q15465|Hedgehog signaling]]**: A cell pathway active during embryonic development, it allows tissue patterning but can be activated in cancers.
- **Hypoxia**: Low oxygen conditions common in densely packed tumor interior, triggers HIF-driven adaptive responses.

## I

- **[[igf-1|IGF signaling]]**: The insulin-like growth factor pathway that promotes growth and survival through PI3K-AKT activation.
- **Immune checkpoints**: Inhibitory receptors such as PD-1 and CTLA-4 that restrain T cells and are targets of cancer therapy.
- **[[immune-surveillance|Immune surveillance]]**: The process by which immune cells detect and remove emerging cancer cells.
- **[[Indeterminate growth|Indeterminate growth]]**: A life-history pattern where organisms continue growing after maturity through continued cell proliferation.
- **Inflammaging**: Chronic, low-grade inflammation that increases with age and contributes to tissue dysfunction.

## K

- **Kinase signaling**: Enzyme-driven phosphorylation cascades (such as MAPK or PI3K-AKT) that transmit growth and survival signals.
- **[[kras-kras-p01116|KRAS]]**: A small GTPase that activates RAF-MEK-ERK signaling when mutated in many cancers.

## L

- **LEGI (local excitation, global inhibition)**: A sensory computation motif that enables gradient detection and adaptation in the model.
- **Loss of heterozygosity (LOH)**: Loss of a functional allele that unmasks recessive tumor suppressor mutations.

## M

- **MAPK pathway**: A signaling cascade (RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK) that promotes proliferation and differentiation downstream of growth factors.
- **Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)**: Enzymes that degrade extracellular matrix to enable remodeling and invasion.
- **Metastasis**: The spread of cancer cells to distant sites via invasion, circulation, and colonization.
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction**: Impaired mitochondrial function that alters metabolism and can promote senescence or apoptosis resistance.
- **Morphogen**: A diffusible signal whose concentration gradient provides positional information to cells.
- **[[mtor-mtor-p42345|mTOR pathway]]**: A nutrient-sensing kinase network controlling growth and metabolism that is often hyperactivated in cancer.
- **Mutation load**: The cumulative number of mutations carried by a cell, used in the model as a DNA damage proxy.

## N

- **Necrosis**: Uncontrolled cell death often associated with membrane rupture and inflammation.
- **NetLogo**: An agent-based modeling environment used for building and exploring the clonal cooperation model.
- **[[notch1-notch1-p46531|Notch signaling]]**: A juxtacrine pathway that controls cell fate decisions and is dysregulated in many cancers.

## O

- **Occupancy guard**: A two-phase movement rule that prevents transient fragmentation by limiting how many cells vacate a patch per tick.
- **[[oncogene-classification|Oncogene]]**: A gene that, when activated or overexpressed, promotes cell proliferation or survival and can drive cancer.
- **Oxidative stress**: An imbalance between reactive oxygen species and antioxidant defenses that damages biomolecules and promotes aging and disease.

## P

- **Passenger mutation**: A genetic change that does not confer a selective advantage but co-occurs with driver mutations.
- **[[p16ink4a-cdkn2a|p16-RB pathway]]**: A tumor-suppressive axis that enforces cell-cycle arrest by inhibiting CDK4/6 and maintaining RB activity.
- **[[p21cip1-cdkn1a|p21]]**: A CDK inhibitor induced by p53 that enforces a damage-responsive cell-cycle arrest.
- **[[p53-tp53|p53 pathway]]**: A master damage-response network that triggers repair, arrest, senescence, or apoptosis depending on context.
- **[[pd-1-pdcd1|PD-1]] / [[pd-l1-cd274|PD-L1]]**: An immune checkpoint pair that suppresses T cell activity and is targeted by cancer immunotherapies.
- **PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway**: A central growth and survival axis activated by growth factors and oncogenic mutations.
- **Polarity (internal angle)**: A compact internal orientation representation that controls movement and division direction in the model.
- **[[principal-agent-problem|Principal-agent problem]]**: The challenge of aligning cellular incentives with organismal goals in multicellular life.
- **Proliferation**: Increase in cell number through division, regulated by checkpoints, growth signals, and crowding.

## Q

- **Quiescence**: A reversible, non-dividing state where cells reduce metabolism and can re-enter the cycle upon cues.

## R

- **[[rb-rb1|RB tumor suppressor]]**: A gatekeeper of the G1-S transition that restrains E2F and enforces cell-cycle arrest.
- **Reaction-diffusion**: Models that combine local reactions and diffusion to produce spatial patterns, often used for morphogen dynamics.
- **[[replicative-credit|Replicative credit]]**: The allocation of resources toward faithful DNA copying as an investment in genomic integrity.
- **Replicative senescence**: Permanent arrest triggered by telomere shortening after repeated divisions.

## S

- **[[cellular-senescence|SASP]]**: The inflammatory and proteolytic secretome produced by senescent cells that remodels tissues.
- **[[scandal-hypothesis|Scandal hypothesis]]**: The view that cancer represents a breakdown of multicellular governance enabling selfish cellular behavior.
- **[[selection-shadow-theory|Selection shadow]]**: The decline in selection pressure with age that allows late-acting deleterious traits to persist.
- **Semelparity**: A life-history strategy where organisms reproduce once and then die, shaping selection on maintenance and repair.
- **Senolytic therapy**: Treatments that selectively eliminate senescent cells to improve tissue function and healthspan.
- **[[shh-shh-q15465|SHH or Hedgehog signaling]]**: A morphogen pathway that patterns development and is often co-opted by tumors.
- **Spatial hashing**: A performance technique that bins agents into grid cells to accelerate neighborhood queries in the model.
- **Stem cell niche**: The specialized microenvironment that maintains stemness and regulates division and differentiation.

## T

- **[[telomeres|Telomeres]]**: Repetitive chromosome ends that shorten with divisions and act as a replication counter and damage signal.
- **[[telomerase-tert|Telomerase]]**: The enzyme that extends telomeres and supports replicative immortality in many cancer cells.
- **[[tgfb1-tgfb1-p01137|TGF-beta signaling]]**: A context-dependent pathway that can suppress early tumor growth yet promote invasion and metastasis later.
- **Tissue bonds**: Explicit links between cells used to maintain organismal connectivity and viability in the model.
- **Time constant (GRN)**: A parameter controlling how quickly transcription factor states integrate inputs toward steady state.
- **Transcription factor (TF)**: A protein that binds DNA to regulate gene expression and transmit signaling to the genome.
- **Transmissible cancer**: A cancer that spreads between individuals via living cancer cells rather than solely by viruses.
- **Tumor microenvironment (TME)**: The ecosystem of cancer, stromal, immune cells, and ECM that shapes tumor behavior.
- **Tumor suppressor**: A gene that prevents uncontrolled growth and whose loss promotes cancer.
- **[[tumor-suppressor-theory-of-aging|Tumor-suppressor theory of aging]]**: The idea that mechanisms preventing cancer, like senescence, contribute to aging by limiting regeneration.

## V

- **[[vegf-vegfa|VEGF signaling]]**: A vascular growth program that drives angiogenesis and is targeted by anti-angiogenic therapies.
- **Venetoclax**: A BCL-2 inhibitor used to restore apoptosis in cancers addicted to anti-apoptotic signaling.

## W

- **[[weismann-barrier|Weismann barrier]]**: The separation between germline and soma that limits inheritance of acquired somatic changes.
- **[[wnt3a-wnt3a-p56704|Wnt signaling]]**: A developmental pathway that stabilizes beta-catenin to drive proliferation and patterning.
- **Winners and losers (cell competition)**: The neighborhood selection dynamic where fitter clones outcompete less-fit neighbors.

## Y

- **[[yap-yap1|YAP]] / [[taz-wwtr1|TAZ]] – Hippo pathway**: Mechanosensitive transcriptional co-activators that promote growth and survival when Hippo signaling is low.
