# AKT1

AKT1 (protein kinase B α) is a serine/threonine kinase that integrates insulin and growth-factor signals into commands for metabolism, proliferation, and survival.

## Function

[PI3K](pik3ca-pik3ca-p42336.md) converts the membrane lipid PIP2 to PIP3; AKT docks at PIP3 through its PH domain and is activated by phosphorylation at two sites (Thr308 by PDK1, Ser473 by mTORC2). Active AKT then phosphorylates a broad substrate set with a shared theme: keep the cell alive and growing. It inactivates the pro-apoptotic proteins BAD and FOXO, drives protein synthesis by inhibiting TSC2 and thereby activating [mTOR](mtor-mtor-p42345.md), and moves glucose transporters to the membrane in response to [insulin](insulin.md). PTEN opposes the whole route by dephosphorylating PIP3.

## In cancer

AKT1 carries the E17K activating mutation in a few percent of breast, colorectal, and ovarian cancers, and AKT1 amplification recurs in gastric cancers. More often, AKT is hyperactivated indirectly — through PIK3CA mutation or PTEN loss. AKT inhibitors have struggled in the clinic for toxicity reasons: the same pathway manages normal glucose homeostasis, so inhibition produces hyperglycemia and rash.

## Notes

- Human gene symbol: AKT1; UniProt P31749; 480 amino acids; AGC kinase family; three isoforms (AKT1/2/3) with partially overlapping roles.
