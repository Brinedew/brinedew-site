# AKT1

AKT1 (protein kinase B α) is the serine/threonine kinase at the center of the insulin and growth-factor survival pathway, integrating signals about nutrients and growth into commands for metabolism, proliferation, and survival.

## Function

[PI3K](pik3ca-pik3ca-p42336.md) converts the membrane lipid PIP2 to PIP3; AKT docks at PIP3 through its PH domain and is activated by phosphorylation at two sites (Thr308 by PDK1, Ser473 by mTORC2). Active AKT then phosphorylates a broad substrate set with a common theme: keep the cell alive and growing. It inactivates the pro-apoptotic proteins BAD and FOXO, activates [mTOR](mtor-mtor-p42345.md)-driven protein synthesis through TSC2 inhibition, and moves glucose transporters to the membrane in response to [insulin](insulin.md). PTEN opposes the whole route by dephosphorylating PIP3.

## In cancer

AKT1 carries the E17K activating mutation in a few percent of breast, colorectal, and ovarian cancers, and AKT1 amplification recurs in gastric cancers. More often, AKT is hyperactivated indirectly — through PIK3CA mutation or PTEN loss. AKT inhibitors have struggled in the clinic for toxicity reasons: the same pathway manages normal glucose homeostasis, so inhibition produces hyperglycemia and rash.
